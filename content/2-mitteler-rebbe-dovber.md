# Rabbi Dovber Schneuri — the "Mitteler Rebbe"

- **English name:** Rabbi Dovber Schneuri (also spelled DovBer, Dov Ber)
- **Hebrew name:** רבי דובער שניאורי — דובער בן שניאור זלמן
- **Honorific / title:** the *Mitteler Rebbe* ("Middle Rebbe," Yiddish), and in Chabad literature *Admur HaEmtzai* (אדמו"ר האמצעי); his maamarim are collectively called *Toras Chaim* / *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai*
- **Life years:** 9 Kislev 5534 – 9 Kislev 5588 (13 November 1773 – 16 December 1827)
- **Generation:** 2nd Rebbe of the Chabad-Lubavitch dynasty
- **Years of leadership:** 5573–5588 (1813–1827), roughly 14 years; the nesius began 24 Tevet 5573 (January 1813), upon the passing of his father, the Alter Rebbe

The Mitteler Rebbe was the second Rebbe of the Chabad-Lubavitch dynasty, son and successor of the founder, Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi (the *Alter Rebbe*, author of the *Tanya*). He took up the leadership in early 1813 (24 Tevet 5573), in the immediate aftermath of his father's passing during the upheaval of Napoleon's 1812 invasion, and settled the movement in the town of Lubavitch — which would give the dynasty its name. He was succeeded by his nephew and son-in-law, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (the *Tzemach Tzedek*), the third Rebbe. His defining contribution to Chabad literature was the vast, systematic *expansion* of Chassidic exposition: where the Alter Rebbe wrote tersely, the Mitteler Rebbe's discourses are famously long, fully elaborated and analytically exhaustive — a style the Rebbeim nicknamed *"rechovos hanahar"* ("the broad expanse of the river"). He was also the dynasty's first great *publisher*, issuing his father's teachings and a new sefer of his own almost every year, and he composed distinct works tailored to different types of students. He was arrested in 1826 on a libelous charge and freed on 10 Kislev 5587 (1 December 1826) — observed in Chabad as his *chag hageulah* (festival of liberation). He is buried in Niezhin (Nizhyn), Ukraine.

## The Corpus at a Glance

The Mitteler Rebbe's output is overwhelmingly **Chassidus** (mystical/philosophical Torah). He delivered an estimated **800+ maamarim** (discourses), which fill roughly **nineteen volumes** today (so the Derher biography; the dedicated Kehot *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai* series published 5745–5751 is the 18-volume core, with related seforim issued separately). His corpus divides into four streams:

1. **Stand-alone chassidic seforim** he wrote and published in his lifetime, each one a sustained treatise on a defined theme — repentance, faith, the Unity, Shema/tefillin, the festivals, the Days of Awe, contemplative prayer. These are the "anchor" works (Shaarei Teshuvah, Derech Chaim, Ner Mitzvah V'Torah Or, Imrei Binah, Ateres Rosh, Shaarei Orah, Toras Chaim).
2. **Collected maamarim** — the comprehensive *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai* series (Kehot) and the parallel *Toras Chaim* parsha series, arranged by Chumash, festivals, Nach, topics, and *kuntreisim*.
3. **Halacha** — *Piskei Dinim*, his rulings on Shulchan Aruch (Yoreh Deah and Even HaEzer).
4. **Letters and "tracts"** — *Igros Kodesh* (collected correspondence) and the imprisonment-era plea *Bad Kodesh*, plus short *kuntresim* on the conduct of prayer (most famously *Kuntres HaHispaalus*).

Because so much was written for the scholarly elite, he also produced one deliberately popular work, *Pokeach Ivrim*, in Yiddish for the ordinary penitent. Note one common confusion: the *Tract on Prayer / Kuntres HaTefillah* is by the fifth Rebbe (Rashab), **not** the Mitteler Rebbe.

Where to read him in the original, free: **Chabad's official library, chabadlibrary.org**, hosts virtually his entire corpus in Hebrew at `chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/` ("adhaam" = *Admur HaEmtzai*). The site is a JavaScript single-page reader, so the **collection root is the reliable entry point** and individual sub-page anchors may differ in the live reader; treat any deep links below as plausible navigation aids rather than guaranteed permalinks. English translations exist for a meaningful subset and are noted per-work below.

## Complete Works

### Halacha

**Piskei Dinim** (פסקי דינים, "Legal Rulings")
- *Type:* Halacha (rulings/commentary on Shulchan Aruch). *Language:* Hebrew.
- *Extent:* One large volume (expanded 2022 Kehot edition, ~944 pp.), covering Yoreh Deah ch. 32–57 and Even HaEzer ch. 119–151.
- *Date:* Authored during his lifetime; modern critical edition by Kehot (2022).
- *Description:* His halachic rulings and Shulchan Aruch commentary — the one strictly-halachic work in his corpus.
- **Original online:** Kehot edition (paid): https://store.kehotonline.com/ (search "Piskei Dinim Admur Haemtzoee"; SKU HMR-PISK). Announcement: https://anash.org/three-seforim-of-the-mitteler-rebbe-released-in-three-months/
- **English:** No full English translation.

### Chassidus — Stand-alone Seforim

**Shaarei Teshuvah** (שערי תשובה, "Gates of Repentance")
- *Type:* Chassidic treatise. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* issued in two sections/parts. *Date:* published 5577–5578 (1816–1818).
- *Description:* On teshuvah, prayer and free will; foundational study text for the month of Elul. (The thematically related *Derech Chaim*, published 5579, is a distinct sefer — not a numbered third volume of this work.)
- **Original online (free):** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/ (Shaarei Teshuvah; deep path /tshu/ — verify in the live reader).
- **English:** No full English translation.

**Derech Chaim** (דרך חיים, "The Way / Path of Life")
- *Type:* Chassidic treatise (thematically related to Shaarei Teshuvah). *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* one volume. *Date:* published 5579 (1818/19); redesigned Kehot edition 2022.
- *Description:* On repentance and self-refinement; one of his most popular works, customarily studied in Elul.
- **Original online:** Kehot new edition (paid): https://store.kehotonline.com/ (search "Derech Chaim Admur Haemtzoee" — verify the exact SKU at the store, as the cited HMR-DERE code is unconfirmed). Hebrew text also at chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/.
- **English:** Yes — *The Path of Life*, trans. by neirot.com: https://neirot.com/product/the-path-of-life/ (product page may bot-block automated requests; also available print-on-demand at Lulu).

**Ner Mitzvah V'Torah Or** (נר מצוה ותורה אור, "A Lamp is the Commandment and the Torah is Light")
- *Type:* Two-part chassidic treatise. *Language:* Hebrew. *Date:* published 5580 (1819/20), Kopust.
- *Structure:* Part 1 = **Shaar HaEmunah** (שער האמונה, "Gate of Faith") on the Exodus, matzah, and the Splitting of the Sea (studied for Pesach); Part 2 = **Shaar HaYichud** (שער היחוד, "Gate of Unity") on *seder hishtalshelus* (the chain of worlds) and how to meditate on it.
- *Description:* His systematic exposition of faith and of the kabbalistic structure of creation — among his most-studied works.
- **Original online (free):** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/ (Ner Mitzvah V'Torah Or; deep path /ner/ — verify in the live reader).
- **English:** Yes (multiple). Shaar HaYichud: *The Gate of Unity*, trans. Rabbi Benjamin Walters (ISBN 978-1467986359), and a translation/elucidation by neirot.com (https://neirot.com/, free PDF: https://neirot.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Shaar-HaYichud-The-Gate-of-Unity-English-Only.pdf). Shaar HaEmunah is included in neirot.com's *Gate of Unity and Faith* PDF (https://neirot.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/The-Gate-of-Unity-and-Faith.pdf).

**Imrei Binah** (אמרי בינה, "Words of Understanding")
- *Type:* Chassidic treatise. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* organized into *shaarim* (gates); Shaar Kerias Shema alone runs ~54 chapters. *Date:* published 5581 (1820/21), Kopust; originally composed for his chossid Yekusiel Liepler.
- *Description:* On the mitzvos of reciting Shema and donning tefillin (and tzitzis) — considered one of the most profound texts in Chabad thought.
- **Original online (free):** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/ (Imrei Binah; deep path /imrei/ — verify in the live reader).
- **English:** Partial only — the opening gateway (*Pesach HaShaar*) appears in English as "The Gateway to Understanding" (truekabbalah.com). No full English translation.

**Ateres Rosh** (עטרת ראש, "Crown of the Head")
- *Type:* Chassidic discourses for the High Holidays. *Language:* Hebrew. *Date:* published 5581 (1820/21).
- *Description:* Discourses on Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Shabbos Shuvah and the Ten Days of Repentance; studied during the Days of Awe.
- **Original online:** Kehot edition (paid): https://store.kehotonline.com/ (search "Ateres Rosh"; SKU HMR-ATER). Hebrew text also at chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/.
- **English:** No full English translation.

**Shaarei Orah** (שערי אורה, "Gates of Light")
- *Type:* Chassidic discourses for the winter festivals. *Language:* Hebrew. *Date:* first printed 5582 (1821/22).
- *Description:* On Chanukah and Purim; called "the aleph-beis of Chassidus" and recommended for study in the Chanukah–Purim season.
- **Original online (free):** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/ (Shaarei Orah; deep path /orah/ — verify in the live reader).
- **English:** Yes — *Gates of Light*, trans. by neirot.com (free PDF: https://neirot.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Shaarei-Orah-Gates-of-Light.pdf). The Chanukah discourse *Ki Atah Neri* is separately published as **Flames** (Kehot Chasidic Heritage Series, trans. Naftali Loewenthal): https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/63273/jewish/Flames.htm

**Toras Chaim** (תורת חיים, "Torah of Life")
- *Type:* Chassidic discourses on the weekly parsha. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* covers Bereishis and Shemos; the modern Kehot set runs to ~3 volumes (Bereishis; Shemos vols. 1–2). *Date:* delivered/published in installments from ~5586 (1825/26).
- *Description:* Parsha-based discourses on Genesis and Exodus; originally released piecemeal so chassidim could afford them. *Toras Chaim* is a distinct titled maamarim series, sometimes counted alongside *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai* as the two parallel collected-maamarim corpora; the name is also used loosely for the broader body of his discourses.
- **Original online (free):** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/ (Toras Chaim; deep path /tch/ — verify in the live reader).
- **English:** No full English translation (selected discourses appear in anthologies).

### Chassidus — Contemplative-Prayer "Tracts" (Kuntresim)

**Kuntres HaHispaalus** (קונטרס ההתפעלות, "Tract on Ecstasy"), also titled **Kuntres HaHisbonenus / HaHitpa'alut**
- *Type:* Short chassidic tract on the psychology of prayer. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* a kuntres (pamphlet/letter). *Date:* early 1810s–1820s.
- *Description:* His classic analysis of *hispaalus* (ecstatic emotional arousal) vs. genuine inner *hisbonenus* (contemplation) in divine service — a defining statement of the Chabad method.
- **Original online (free):** included in *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai – Kuntreisim* at chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/maamarei/; also on Sefaria as *Kuntres HaHitpa'alut* (https://www.sefaria.org/).
- **English:** Yes — *Tract on Ecstasy*, trans. & introduced by Louis Jacobs (Vallentine Mitchell, London, 1963). Free PDF: https://booksof.louisjacobs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Tract-on-Ecstasy-web.pdf

**Pokeach Ivrim** (פוקח עורים, "Opening the Eyes of the Blind")
- *Type:* Popular ethical/teshuvah guide. *Language:* **Yiddish** (his only major popular-language work). *Date:* composed during his lifetime (early Shklov/Kopust printings; later eds. incl. Shklov 1833 and Shanghai 1943).
- *Description:* Plain-language moral exhortations and guidance for the *baal teshuvah* and ordinary Jew — written for those who could not access his scholarly seforim; includes his practice of reciting Tehillim (esp. Ps. 51) before prayer.
- **Original online (free):** within *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai – Kuntreisim* at chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/maamarei/ (exact sub-page varies in the live reader); also on HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full English translation.

### Chassidus — Collected Maamarim

**Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai** (מאמרי אדמו"ר האמצעי, "Discourses of the Mitteler Rebbe")
- *Type:* Comprehensive collected discourses. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* the dedicated Kehot series is **18 volumes**, arranged by Chumash (Bereishis–Devarim), Nach, *Inyonim* (topics), *Drushei Chasunah* (wedding discourses) and *Kuntreisim*; counting the parallel *Toras Chaim* parsha series, the Derher describes his maamarim as occupying **nineteen volumes**. *Date:* delivered 1813–1827; critically edited and published by Kehot 5745–5751 (1984/85–1990/91).
- *Description:* The master collection of his 800+ maamarim, drawn from manuscripts and chozrim's transcripts; supersedes piecemeal earlier printings.
- **Original online:** Kehot editions (paid): https://store.kehotonline.com/ (e.g. *Kuntresim*, SKU HMR-SMK); index of the maamarim at chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/maamarei/.
- **English:** No full English translation (individual discourses translated in the Kehot *Chasidic Heritage Series*, e.g. *Flames*).
- *Reference tool:* **Mafteach Sifrei Maamarei u'Drushei Admur HaEmtzai** (bibliography/index by R. Alexander Zissel Piekarsky, Kehot 2022) — chronological & alphabetical index of all his discourses and their printings.

**Maamarim Yekarim** (מאמרים יקרים, "Precious Discourses")
- *Type:* Collection of select discourses. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* a single collection.
- *Date:* Published posthumously; listed among his works on Chabadpedia.
- *Description:* An anthology of select maamarim of the Mitteler Rebbe, preserved and printed separately from the main Kehot series.
- **Original online:** Hebrew scans on HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full English translation.

### Liturgy / Commentary on Prayer

**Pirush HaMilos** (פירוש המלות, "Explanation of the Words [of Prayer]")
- *Type:* Chassidic commentary on the liturgy/Siddur. *Language:* Hebrew. *Date:* compiled from his discourses; printed posthumously.
- *Description:* Word-by-word chassidic exposition of the prayers (e.g. *Nishmas Kol Chai*). (Note: Chabad's liturgical-commentary tradition spans the Alter Rebbe, this work, and the Tzemach Tzedek's later additions; attribution of specific passages can be layered.)
- **Original online (free):** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/ (Pirush HaMilos; deep path /pirush/ — verify in the live reader).
- **English:** No full English translation.

### Edited / Published by Him (not original authorship)

**Siddur with Chassidic Commentary** ("Siddur im Da"ch")
- *Type:* Annotated prayer book. *Language:* Hebrew. *Date:* published 5576 (1815/16).
- *Description:* The Alter Rebbe's discourses on the prayers, transcribed and arranged by the Mitteler Rebbe — one of his earliest publishing acts.
- **Original online:** Hebrew editions via Kehot / HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full English translation of this annotated edition.

**Biurei HaZohar** (ביאורי הזהר, "Elucidations of the Zohar")
- *Type:* Discourses on Zohar passages. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* one volume (Kehot revised ed., ~608 pp.). *Date:* first published ~1816; Kehot revised edition after decades out of print.
- *Description:* **The Alter Rebbe's** discourses on the Zohar that the Mitteler Rebbe heard on Shabbos evenings and committed to print — i.e., he is the editor/publisher, not the author. (A separate *Biurei HaZohar* was later authored by the Tzemach Tzedek — do not conflate the two.)
- **Original online:** Kehot edition (paid): https://store.kehotonline.com/ (search "Biurei HaZohar"; SKU HMR-BIUR).
- **English:** No full English translation.

**The Alter Rebbe's Torah** — in his first three years he also published nine volumes of his father's works, including the *Tanya* and *Igeres HaKodesh*, the *Shaar HaYichud V'HaEmunah* material, and the *Shulchan Aruch HaRav* (publisher/editor role).

### Letters — Igros

**Igros Kodesh — Admur HaEmtzai** (אגרות קודש, "Holy Letters")
- *Type:* Collected correspondence. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* one volume (~406 pp., Kehot). *Date:* written across his leadership; collected and published by Kehot.
- *Description:* His letters of guidance and communal direction — including directives on prayer and Torah study, the Chevron settlement, and the agricultural colonies near Cherson; also includes the well-known letters written during his German journey and his imprisonment-era correspondence.
- **Original online (free):** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/igrot/ (single-volume collection; exact sub-page varies in the live reader).
- **English:** No full English collection (individual letters translated and quoted in secondary works such as the *Derher* biography).

**Bad Kodesh** (בד קודש, "Holy Linen")
- *Type:* Petition/letter (published as a sefer). *Language:* Hebrew. *Date:* written during his imprisonment, 5587 (1826).
- *Description:* His formal letter to the governor of the Vitebsk district, written from confinement, pleading for a just judgment — directly tied to the *chag hageulah* narrative. One of the few of his writings with a full English rendering.
- **Original online:** Hebrew via Kehot / HebrewBooks; listed among his works on Chabadpedia.
- **English:** Yes — *A Judgment of Truth and Mercy*, trans. Rabbis Eliyahu Touger & Sholom Ber Wineberg.

## Scope & Caveats

- **What "complete" means here.** This catalog lists the recognized, published body of the Mitteler Rebbe's work: his stand-alone seforim, the collected *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai* and *Toras Chaim* series, his halachic *Piskei Dinim*, his Siddur/Zohar editorial works, and his *Igros Kodesh* / *Bad Kodesh*. The single largest category — his 800+ maamarim — is not itemized title-by-title here; it is consolidated in the Kehot series and indexed in the *Mafteach* bibliography.
- **Volume counts.** The dedicated Kehot *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai* series (5745–5751) is an 18-volume set; the Derher biography describes his maamarim as occupying "nineteen volumes" (counting the parallel *Toras Chaim* parsha series). Earlier drafts of this catalog cited "~20 volumes," which overshoots the sources — use "eighteen/nineteen" as the working figure. The *Igros Kodesh* is a single volume (~406 pp.), not two.
- **Still being released / re-edited.** Kehot continues to issue newly typeset, annotated critical editions (e.g. the 2022 *Derech Chaim*, the expanded *Piskei Dinim*, and the *Mafteach* index), so the published corpus is not static.
- **Manuscript material.** Few of his original *kisvei yad* (autograph manuscripts) survive; much of the published text rests on transcripts by *chozrim* (designated reciters) and on copies he sent to communities. Some discourses remain in manuscript or scattered across collections and are not yet fully collated.
- **English translation is partial.** Only a subset is fully translated: *Tract on Ecstasy* (Jacobs), *The Gate of Unity* and *Gate of Faith*, *Gates of Light*, *Derech Chaim / The Path of Life*, the Chanukah discourse *Flames*, and *Bad Kodesh / A Judgment of Truth and Mercy*. Major works — *Imrei Binah* (only the opening gate), *Toras Chaim*, *Ateres Rosh*, *Shaarei Teshuvah*, *Piskei Dinim*, the full *Maamarei Admur HaEmtzai*, and the *Igros* — have **no full English translation**.
- **Common misattributions to avoid.** *Kuntres HaTefillah / Tract on Prayer* is by the fifth Rebbe (Rashab), not the Mitteler Rebbe; and *Biurei HaZohar* exists in two distinct works (the Mitteler Rebbe's edition of his father's discourses vs. the Tzemach Tzedek's own later commentary).
- **Link reliability.** chabadlibrary.org/books/adhaam/ is Chabad's official, free digital home for the Hebrew originals, but it is a JavaScript single-page reader that returns an identical app-shell (HTTP 200) for every path — so individual deep links cannot be confirmed by status code and specific sub-page anchors may differ in the live reader. Cite the collection root and navigate from there. Kehot (store.kehotonline.com) sells the authoritative print/critical editions, though exact product SKUs migrate and should be verified at the store; neirot.com and Louis Jacobs's site host the principal free English translations (the neirot.com product pages may bot-block automated requests but render normally in a browser).
