# Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi — The Alter Rebbe / Baal HaTanya

- **English name:** Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi
- **Hebrew name:** רבי שניאור זלמן מליאדי (Rabbeinu Shneur Zalman ben Baruch)
- **Honorifics / titles:** "Alter Rebbe" (Yiddish: the Old Rebbe), "Admur HaZaken" (אדמו"ר הזקן), "Baal HaTanya" (author of the Tanya), "Baal HaTanya VeHaShulchan Aruch," "the Rav"
- **Life years:** 18 Elul 5505 – 24 Tevet 5573 (September 4, 1745 – December 15, 1812)
- **Generation:** 1st Rebbe of the Chabad-Lubavitch dynasty
- **Years of leadership:** Appointed Maggid of Liozna c. 1767; the leading figure on the ground among the chassidim of Reisin / White Russia after Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk made aliyah in 1777 (Menachem Mendel continued to lead by correspondence from Eretz Yisrael); became the acknowledged Rebbe / leader after Menachem Mendel's death in 1788, until his own passing in 1812.

## Biographical Orientation

Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi was the founder of Chabad (an acronym for *Chochmah–Binah–Daat*), the intellectual school of Hasidism that turned the broad emotional piety of the Baal Shem Tov into a rigorous, systematic theology meant to be grasped by the mind. He was the youngest and most analytic disciple of Rabbi Dovber, the Maggid of Mezeritch (the Baal Shem Tov's successor), and after the Maggid's passing in 1772 he was appointed Maggid of Liozna and became the leading Hasidic figure on the ground in White Russia. Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk, however, remained the recognized leader of the region's chassidim even after his 1777 emigration to the Land of Israel, directing them by correspondence; the Alter Rebbe became the acknowledged Rebbe only after Menachem Mendel's death in 1788. Twice arrested by the Czarist authorities on libelous charges (his release on 19 Kislev 5559/1798 is still celebrated by Chabad as the "New Year of Chassidus"), he relocated to the town of Liadi, by which he is known. His defining contribution is twofold and unmatched among the Rebbes: he authored both the foundational philosophical text of Chabad — the **Tanya** — and a complete recodification of Jewish law, the **Shulchan Aruch HaRav**, making him the only Chabad Rebbe to compose a major halachic code. He fled the 1812 Napoleonic invasion of Russia and died en route in the village of Piena (near Kursk); he is buried in Hadiach (Haditch), Ukraine. He was succeeded by his son, Rabbi Dovber, the "Mitteler Rebbe."

## The Corpus at a Glance

The Alter Rebbe's output is unusually broad and divides cleanly into four genres. (1) **Halacha** — chiefly the *Shulchan Aruch HaRav*, a full code of law written in lucid Hebrew with reasoning, plus *Hilchot Talmud Torah*, *Birkat HaNehenin* (also printed separately as the *Luach Birkat HaNehenin*), *Piskei HaSiddur*, and the responsum-treatise *Amira LeNochri*. (2) **Chassidus / philosophy** — the *Tanya*, the single most important systematic work; the vast body of his oral *maamarim* (Hasidic discourses) delivered over roughly four decades and later collected by Kehot into a multi-volume series organized both by year (the critical year-volumes run 5562–5572 / 1801–1812, though his discourses date from the 1770s onward) and by topic; *Biurei HaZohar* (his expositions on the Zohar); and the choicest discourses arranged by his grandson the Tzemach Tzedek into *Torah Or* and *Likkutei Torah*. (3) **Liturgy** — the *Siddur* ("Siddur HaRav" / *Torah Or* / *Im Dach*), a corrected prayer text in Nusach Ari that became the Chabad standard. (4) **Letters** — the *Igrot Kodesh*, with further letters preserved in anthologies such as *Me'ah She'arim* and *Boneh Yerushalayim*. Much of the corpus is freely available in the original Hebrew online (Chabad.org, Sefaria, HebrewBooks, chabadlibrary.org); English translation is complete for the Tanya, advanced but incomplete for the Shulchan Aruch and the maamarim, and partial or absent for the rest. Note: the maamarim were delivered orally and transcribed by disciples (*hanachot*), so that part of the corpus is editorial reconstruction, and new volumes are still being released.

## Complete Works

### Halacha (Jewish Law)

**שולחן ערוך הרב — Shulchan Aruch HaRav** ("The Rav's Code of Jewish Law")
- *Type:* Code of Jewish law with reasoning. *Language:* Hebrew.
- *Extent / scope:* Originally intended to cover all four parts of Karo's Shulchan Aruch; only **Orach Chaim survives intact**, with fragments of Yoreh Deah, Choshen Mishpat and Even HaEzer (roughly a third of the planned whole). Much was lost in the Lyady fire of 1810. Standard Hebrew edition is ~6–8 volumes.
- *Date:* Begun c. 1770, commissioned by the Maggid of Mezeritch when the author was in his twenties; first complete printing posthumously by his sons in **Kopust (Kopys), 1814–1816**. (Individual sections appeared in print during his lifetime: *Hilchot Talmud Torah*, Shklov 1794; *Luach Birkat HaNehenin*, Shklov 1800.)
- *Description:* A complete restatement of practical halacha that, unusually for a code, supplies the underlying reasoning; accepted as authoritative well beyond Chabad. Often printed together with the author's *Hilchot Talmud Torah*, *Birkat HaNehenin* (laws of blessings), *Kuntres Acharon* (halachic notes/responsa), and *Piskei HaSiddur*.
- **Original online:** Free full Hebrew text on Sefaria (https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh_HaRav) and Chabad.org (https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/3127874/jewish/Shulchan-Aruch-Harav.htm); scans on HebrewBooks (https://hebrewbooks.org).
- **English:** Kehot bilingual Hebrew/English edition (translated/annotated by Rabbi Eliyahu Touger, Rabbi Uri Kaploun, and Rabbi Sholom B. Wineberg), in progress since 2002 — Orach Chaim is essentially complete across roughly 12 volumes (numbered 1–11 and 13); sold at https://store.kehotonline.com/products.asp?dept=460. Partial community English on Sefaria. (Note: shulchanaruchharav.com is a free English summary/digest of the rulings, not a translation of the SAH text itself.)

**הלכות תלמוד תורה — Hilchot Talmud Torah** ("Laws of Torah Study")
- *Type:* Halachic monograph. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* 4 chapters. *Date:* First printed Shklov, 1794 (composed somewhat earlier); often printed within the Shulchan Aruch HaRav.
- *Description:* Codifies the obligation and laws of Torah study, blending halacha with Chassidic perspective.
- **Original online:** Free on Sefaria and Chabad.org (within Shulchan Aruch HaRav).
- **English:** Translated within the Kehot Shulchan Aruch edition; partial English on Sefaria.

**לוח / סדר ברכת הנהנין — Luach Birkat HaNehenin / Seder Birkat HaNehenin** ("Laws of Blessings over Enjoyments")
- *Type:* Halachic monograph (laws of blessings on food and other enjoyments). *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* A short, self-contained code. *Date:* First printed Shklov, 1800; also incorporated into the Shulchan Aruch HaRav and printed with the Siddur.
- *Description:* The Alter Rebbe's concise, practical code of the laws of berachot, widely used as a standalone reference.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew within the Shulchan Aruch HaRav / Siddur texts on Chabad.org and Sefaria; scans on HebrewBooks.
- **English:** Translated within the Kehot Shulchan Aruch edition; no standalone popular English.

**פסקי הסידור — Piskei HaSiddur** ("Halachic Rulings of the Siddur")
- *Type:* Halachic rulings embedded in / derived from the Siddur. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* A set of short rulings printed with the Siddur. *Date:* Compiled from the Alter Rebbe's Siddur (early 19th c.).
- *Description:* The practical halachic decisions the Alter Rebbe built into his prayer book, treated as a discrete item in his halachic corpus.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew with the Siddur on Chabad.org; scans on HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full standalone English.

**אמירה לנכרי — Amira LeNochri** ("Instructing a Non-Jew [on Shabbat]")
- *Type:* Halachic treatise. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* A short monograph. *Date:* Attributed to the Alter Rebbe (precise dating uncertain).
- *Description:* On the laws of *amira le-nochri* — instructing a non-Jew to perform labor on Shabbat — listed among the Alter Rebbe's works in standard Chabad bibliographies (e.g., Chabadpedia).
- **Original online:** Hebrew scans on HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No standalone English. *(Attribution is traditional; treat as attributed.)*

**שו"ת — She'elot u-Teshuvot (Responsa)**
- *Type:* Halachic responsa. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* A small body. *Date:* Across his career.
- *Description:* A limited collection of responsa; some printed within the Shulchan Aruch HaRav / Kuntres Acharon.
- **Original online:** Hebrew on HebrewBooks and chabadlibrary.org.
- **English:** No standalone English.

### Chassidus — Foundational Work

**תניא / ליקוטי אמרים — Tanya / Likutei Amarim** ("It Has Been Taught" / "Collected Sayings")
- *Type:* Systematic Hasidic philosophy/ethics. *Language:* Hebrew.
- *Extent:* Five parts — (1) *Sefer shel Beinonim* / Likutei Amarim ("Book of the Intermediates," 53 ch.); (2) *Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah* ("Gate of Unity and Faith," 12 ch.); (3) *Igeret HaTeshuvah* ("Letter of Repentance," 12 ch.); (4) *Igeret HaKodesh* ("Holy Letter," 32 sections); (5) *Kuntres Acharon* ("Final Treatise," 9 essays).
- *Date:* First printed in **Slavita, 5557** (printing completed 20 Kislev 5557 = December 1796; the Hebrew year 5557 corresponds to 1796/1797). The Slavita first edition contained **only Parts 1–2** (Likutei Amarim and Shaar HaYichud VehaEmunah). *Igeret HaTeshuvah* (Part 3) was added in a subsequent edition (the three-part form is commonly cited from the 1806 Shklov printing). The complete five-part Tanya — adding *Igeret HaKodesh* and *Kuntres Acharon* — was published posthumously in **1814 (Shklov)** by the author's sons.
- *Description:* The "bible of Chabad Chassidus" — a handbook of the inner spiritual life mapping the soul, the *beinoni* (intermediate) ideal, divine unity, repentance, and the mystical meaning of the commandments.
- **Original online:** Free full Hebrew on Chabad.org (https://www.chabad.org/library/tanya/tanya_cdo/aid/1028/jewish/Tanya.htm), Sefaria (https://www.sefaria.org/Tanya), HebrewBooks, chabadlibrary.org.
- **English:** Fully translated. Standard bilingual Kehot edition (Nissan Mindel, with Jacob I. Schochet, Zalman Posner) — free online with "Lessons in Tanya" commentary at https://www.chabad.org/library/tanya/tanya_cdo/aid/1028/ and on Sefaria; print at https://store.kehotonline.com/. Also Rabbi Adin Steinsaltz's *Opening the Tanya* / *Learning from the Tanya* series.

### Chassidus — Maamarim (Hasidic Discourses)

**מאמרי אדמו"ר הזקן — Maamarei Admur HaZaken** ("Discourses of the Alter Rebbe")
- *Type:* Collected Hasidic discourses. *Language:* Hebrew (with Yiddish phrasings in some *hanachot*).
- *Extent:* A Kehot critical series of roughly **30+ volumes**, organized two ways — **by year**: the critical year-volumes run 5562, 5563, 5564, 5565, 5566, 5567, 5568, 5569, 5570, 5571, 5572 (= 1801/2–1811/12), several years in 2 vols each; and **by topic/source**: *Al HaTorah* (Parshiyot — Bereishit & Shemot; Vayikra, Bamidbar & Devarim), *Nevi'im u-Ketuvim*, *Maamarei Razal* (on Talmud, Zohar, Tefillah), *Inyanim*, *Ketuvim*, *Hanachot HaRap*, *Et'halech Liozna* (early discourses), the *HaKtzarim* volume ("the short discourses," a distinct Kehot volume), and *Kuntreisim*. Note: although the year-organized critical volumes start at 5562, the Alter Rebbe's oral teaching spans from the 1770s onward — the earliest discourses are collected separately (Et'halech Liozna, Hanachot HaRap).
- *Date:* Delivered orally c. 1770s–1812, transcribed by disciples; edited and published by Kehot through the 20th–21st centuries (ongoing).
- *Description:* The raw, year-by-year body of the Alter Rebbe's oral teaching from which Torah Or/Likkutei Torah were later distilled.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew on chabadlibrary.org and HebrewBooks; many volumes on Sefaria.
- **English:** No complete English translation; selected discourses translated in various Kehot/SIE booklets and in *Lessons in Sefer HaMaamarim*.

**ביאורי הזוהר — Biurei HaZohar** ("Elucidations of the Zohar")
- *Type:* Hasidic discourses explicating passages of the Zohar. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* 1 work (multiple discourses).
- *Date:* Discourses by the Alter Rebbe, compiled and first published by his son the **Mitteler Rebbe** (early 19th c.). *(Note: the Mitteler Rebbe also authored his own, separate and larger work titled* Biurei HaZohar*; the present entry refers to the Alter Rebbe's discourses that the Mitteler Rebbe brought to print.)*
- *Description:* The Alter Rebbe's mystical expositions on selected Zohar passages, an important bridge between his maamarim and the classical Kabbalah.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew on chabadlibrary.org and HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full English translation.

**תורה אור — Torah Or** ("Light of Torah")
- *Type:* Curated Hasidic discourses on the Torah. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* 1 volume covering Bereishit and Shemot, plus Chanukah, Purim, Shavuot, Pesach.
- *Date:* Discourses delivered by the Alter Rebbe; selected and arranged by his grandson the **Tzemach Tzedek**; first printed **Kopust (Kopys), 5597/1837**.
- *Description:* The first authorized anthology of the Alter Rebbe's discourses, ordered by the weekly parsha.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew on Chabad.org, Sefaria (https://www.sefaria.org/Torah_Ohr), chabadlibrary.org, HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full translation; English adaptations/elucidations of selected maamarim, and *Selections from Torah Or & Likkutei Torah* (Sichos In English, https://shop.sie.org).

**ליקוטי תורה — Likkutei Torah** ("Gleanings of Torah")
- *Type:* Curated Hasidic discourses on the Torah (companion/sequel to Torah Or). *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* Covers Vayikra, Bamidbar, Devarim, Shir HaShirim, and the festivals (incl. Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot).
- *Date:* Selected/arranged by the Tzemach Tzedek; first printed **Zhitomir, 5608/1848**.
- *Description:* The companion volume to Torah Or; together they are the classic "Torah Or u-Likkutei Torah" learned in Chabad.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew on Chabad.org, Sefaria (https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Torah), chabadlibrary.org, HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full translation; selections adapted by SIE; *Lessons in Likkutei Torah* audio/text on learnchassidus.com.

### Liturgy

**סידור (תורה אור / עם דא"ח) — Siddur Torah Or / Seder Tefillot / Siddur Im Dach** ("Prayer Book")
- *Type:* Prayer book (text + Hasidic commentary). *Language:* Hebrew/Aramaic liturgy.
- *Extent:* The base "Siddur HaRav" (Nusach Ari); the expanded *Seder Tefillot Mikol HaShanah / Siddur Im Dach* ("Siddur with Chassidus") adds the Alter Rebbe's discourses on the prayers, printed by Kehot in **2 volumes** (~1,360 pp.).
- *Date:* Compiled by the Alter Rebbe (text fixed from many manuscripts of the Arizal's rite); first printings from c. 1803; *Im Dach* discourses collected later.
- *Description:* The corrected Nusach Ari prayer text that became the standard Chabad siddur; the *Im Dach* edition turns the siddur into a work of Chassidus. The companion commentary *Shaar HaKollel* (by R. Avraham Dovid Lavut) explains the Rebbe's textual rulings.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew text on Chabad.org and Sefaria (https://www.sefaria.org/Siddur_Torah_Or); HebrewBooks.
- **English:** The liturgy is fully translated in Kehot's bilingual *Siddur Tehillat Hashem* (https://store.kehotonline.com); the *Im Dach* Chassidus is largely untranslated.

### Letters

**אגרות קודש (אדמו"ר הזקן) — Igrot Kodesh** ("Holy Letters")
- *Type:* Collected correspondence. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* **183 letters** in 1 volume (~636 pp. with introduction).
- *Date:* Letters span **1781–1811**; collected and published by Kehot (ISBN 978-0-8266-5530-1).
- *Description:* Letters on supporting the early Hasidic settlers in the Holy Land, improving conditions for Russian Jewry, the importance of prayer, foundational Chabad doctrine, and relations with the Lithuanian rabbinic establishment.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew on chabadlibrary.org and HebrewBooks; print available through Kehot (kehot.com / store.kehotonline.com — the older prodinfo.asp links may be stale as the store migrates).
- **English:** No complete published translation; individual letters translated across Chabad.org and scholarly works.

**מאה שערים — Me'ah She'arim** ("One Hundred Gates")
- *Type:* Anthology of the Alter Rebbe's letters/teachings. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* 1 volume.
- *Date:* Compiled and published by R. Chaim Eliezer Bichovsky (early 20th c.).
- *Description:* An early collection preserving letters and teachings of the Alter Rebbe, listed among his works in standard Chabad bibliographies (Chabadpedia, Wikipedia).
- **Original online:** Hebrew scans on HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full English translation.

**בונה ירושלים — Boneh Yerushalayim** ("Builder of Jerusalem")
- *Type:* Collection of maamarim and letters of the first three Rebbeim. *Language:* Hebrew. *Extent:* 1 volume.
- *Date:* Compiled and published by R. Chaim Eliezer Bichovsky (early 20th c., Jerusalem).
- *Description:* An early anthology that preserved discourses and correspondence of the Alter Rebbe (with the Mitteler Rebbe and Tzemach Tzedek) before the full Kehot critical editions.
- **Original online:** Free Hebrew scan on HebrewBooks.
- **English:** No full English translation.

### Other / Attributed

- **קונטרס הרב / קונטרסים — Kuntres HaRav and related short treatises:** Short treatises listed among the Alter Rebbe's works (e.g., Kuntres HaRav). Hebrew on HebrewBooks / chabadlibrary.org; no standalone English.
- **Niggunim (melodies):** Not a written work, but part of his legacy — most famously *the Alter Rebbe's Niggun* (the "Daled Bavos"), reserved for special occasions. Recordings widely available via Chabad.org and Nichoach.

## Scope & Caveats

- **What "complete" means here:** This catalog covers every major published category of the Alter Rebbe's output — halacha (the Shulchan Aruch HaRav and its satellite codes: Hilchot Talmud Torah, Birkat HaNehenin, Piskei HaSiddur, Amira LeNochri, responsa), the Tanya, the maamarim (year-series and topical, including Biurei HaZohar), the curated Torah Or/Likkutei Torah, the Siddur, and the letters (Igrot Kodesh, Me'ah She'arim, Boneh Yerushalayim). It does not enumerate every individual discourse, gloss, or one-off responsum.
- **Loss and reconstruction:** Much of the *Shulchan Aruch HaRav* (Yoreh Deah, Choshen Mishpat, Even HaEzer) was lost in the Lyady fire (1810) and never recovered; only Orach Chaim survives intact (roughly a third of the planned whole). The maamarim were oral teachings preserved through students' transcriptions (*hanachot*), so editions reflect editorial reconstruction and occasional variant versions. The Tanya's first-edition date is given as 5557 (printing completed 20 Kislev 5557 = December 1796), with the Hebrew year corresponding to 1796/1797.
- **Authorship vs. compilation:** Several works in the corpus were arranged or brought to print by others — *Torah Or* and *Likkutei Torah* by the Tzemach Tzedek, *Biurei HaZohar* (the Alter Rebbe's discourses) by the Mitteler Rebbe, and *Me'ah She'arim* / *Boneh Yerushalayim* by R. Chaim Eliezer Bichovsky. A few items (e.g., *Amira LeNochri*) rest on traditional attribution and are flagged as such above.
- **Still being released / translated:** Kehot's critical *Maamarei Admur HaZaken* series and the bilingual *Shulchan Aruch HaRav* are both ongoing multi-decade projects with volumes still appearing. English coverage is uneven: the **Tanya is fully translated and free online**; the **Shulchan Aruch** is far along but not finished; **Torah Or, Likkutei Torah, the maamarim, Biurei HaZohar, the Im Dach Siddur, and the Igrot Kodesh have only partial or no full English translation**, so a serious learner will rely on the free Hebrew originals (Chabad.org, Sefaria, HebrewBooks, chabadlibrary.org) for most of the corpus beyond the Tanya.
- **Verify counts and links:** Volume counts for the maamarim and Shulchan Aruch sets shift as new printings appear; numbers above are "approx." where the series is incomplete. Bare-domain HebrewBooks citations indicate the host carries the text but are not direct item links; Kehot store URLs are migrating between store.kehotonline.com and kehot.com/kehot.org, so specific product links may go stale.
