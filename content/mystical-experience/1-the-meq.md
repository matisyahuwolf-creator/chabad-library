# The Mystical Experience Questionnaire (MEQ30)

The MEQ30 is the standard self-report measure of mystical-type experience in psychedelic
research. A participant fills it in after a session, rating 30 statements about what they
experienced. This page covers where it came from, what it measures, how it is scored, the scales
used beside it, and its known weaknesses.

Tags: **VERIFIED** = abstract or full text read; **PARTLY** = metadata confirmed, some content
from a secondary source. Full references are in the [bibliography](bibliography.md).

---

## 1. Lineage: from a philosopher's categories to a 30-item scale

| Year | Step | What it contributed |
|---|---|---|
| 1960 | **W. T. Stace**, *Mysticism and Philosophy* | A "common core" of mystical experience drawn from religious and historical accounts: internal unity, external unity, nontemporal/nonspatial quality, a sense of life in all things, noetic quality, sacredness, peace and joy, paradoxicality, ineffability. Stace wrote that mystics take "the One which is disclosed in the introvertive experience" to be "identical with the One which is disclosed in the extrovertive experience" (quoted in MacLean et al. 2012). **PARTLY**: categories as summarised by MacLean et al. |
| 1962–63 | **Walter Pahnke**, Good Friday Experiment and Harvard dissertation | Eight categories (unity, transcendence of time and space, sacredness, objective reality, positive mood, ineffability, paradoxicality, transiency) and a "complete" criterion: "the total score and the score in each separate category must be at least 60 to 70 percent." **PARTLY**, via Doblin 1991 |
| 1975 | **Ralph Hood**, Mysticism Scale (M-scale) | 32 items, four for each of eight Stace dimensions; measures *lifetime* experience, not one session. Hood 1975 proposed two factors; the familiar three (introvertive, extrovertive, interpretation) come from Hood, Morris & Watson 1993 and were confirmed in US and Iranian samples (Hood et al. 2001). **PARTLY** |
| 2006 | **MEQ43** (Pahnke–Richards), in Griffiths et al. | 43 items hidden among 57 distractors in the 100-item States of Consciousness Questionnaire; seven subscales. **VERIFIED** |
| 2012 | **MacLean, Leoutsakos, Johnson & Griffiths** | Factor analysis of 1,602 + 440 online reports of psilocybin-mushroom experiences kept 30 of 43 items in four factors. Both paradoxicality items dropped out. The authors call the merging of internal unity, external unity, noetic quality and sacredness into one factor "novel." **VERIFIED** |
| 2015 | **Barrett, Johnson & Griffiths** | Validated the MEQ30 in 184 laboratory psilocybin sessions (≥20 mg/70 kg) pooled from five studies, and published a stand-alone version. **VERIFIED** (full text) |

## 2. What the MEQ30 measures

Each item is rated on a six-point scale: 0 "none; not at all", 1 "so slight cannot decide",
2 "slight", 3 "moderate", 4 "strong (equivalent in degree to any previous strong experience or
expectation of this description)", 5 "extreme (more than ever before in my life and stronger
than 4)" (Barrett et al. 2015). **VERIFIED**

| Factor | Items | α (lab sample) | Example items |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mystical** (internal unity, external unity, noetic quality, sacredness) | 15 | .97 | "Freedom from the limitations of your personal self and feeling a unity or bond with what was felt to be greater than your personal self." · "Experience of the insight that 'all is One'." · "Feeling that you experienced something profoundly sacred and holy." · "You are convinced now, as you look back on your experience, that in it you encountered ultimate reality." |
| **Positive mood** | 6 | .92 | "Experience of amazement." · "Feelings of peace and tranquility." · "Sense of awe or awesomeness." |
| **Transcendence of time and space** | 6 | .86 | "Loss of your usual sense of time." · "Sense of being 'outside of' time, beyond past and future." · "Being in a realm with no space boundaries." |
| **Ineffability** | 3 | .90 | "Sense that the experience cannot be described adequately in words." · "Feeling that it would be difficult to communicate your own experience to others who have not had similar experiences." |

Factor sizes and alphas: **VERIFIED** (Barrett et al. 2015). Item wording: **PARTLY**, as printed
in Schenberg et al. 2017, Table 3, with SOCQ item numbers that match Barrett et al. The official
stand-alone form is Appendix 2 of Barrett et al. 2015; check wording against it before reuse.

### Scoring and the "complete mystical experience"

Each factor is scored as the mean (or percentage of the maximum) of its items. The cut-off, in
the authors' words:

> "Observations on the MEQ30 that had a score ≥60% of the maximum possible score on each of the
> four subscales of the MEQ30 were considered a 'complete mystical experience.'"
> (Barrett et al. 2015) **VERIFIED**

This continues Pahnke's 60–70% rule. In the earlier MEQ43, the higher of internal or external
unity counted, so six of seven subscales had to pass.

### What it predicted in the validation study

In Barrett et al. (2015), the MEQ30 total predicted all four persisting-effect ratings
(personal meaning, spiritual significance, well-being, behaviour change) at follow-up. A single
overall drug-intensity item predicted none of them. This is the core of the claim that the
*kind* of experience, not just its strength, matters. **VERIFIED**

## 3. Sister scales

| Scale | What it adds | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **Hood Mysticism Scale (M-scale)** | Lifetime mystical experience; separates experience from religious interpretation | Hood 1975; Hood et al. 2001 |
| **Ego Dissolution Inventory (EDI)** | 8 items on loss of the sense of self; correlates ρ = .735 with MEQ unity items | Nour et al. 2016 **VERIFIED** |
| **5D-ASC / 11D-ASC** | Broad altered-state profile: oceanic boundlessness, dread of ego dissolution, visionary restructuralisation, etc.; the 11 lower-order scales are measurement-invariant across drugs and settings | Dittrich 1998; Studerus et al. 2010 **VERIFIED** |
| **Challenging Experience Questionnaire (CEQ)** | Seven factors: grief, fear, death, insanity, isolation, physical distress, paranoia | Barrett et al. 2016 **VERIFIED** |
| **Emotional Breakthrough Inventory (EBI)** | 6 items on emotional release and resolution | Roseman et al. 2019 **VERIFIED** |
| **Persisting Effects Questionnaire (PEQ)** | Rated meaning (1–8), spiritual significance, well-being (−3 to +3), behaviour change (0–5) weeks or months later | Griffiths et al. 2006 onward **VERIFIED** |
| **Awe Experience Scale (AWE-S)** | Six factors: time, self-loss, connectedness, vastness, physical sensations, accommodation | Yaden et al. 2019 **PARTLY** |

Note: the large COMP360 depression trials did not use the MEQ at all. They used the 5D-ASC and
the EBI (Goodwin et al. 2025). **VERIFIED**

## 4. Known weaknesses

From the developers themselves (**VERIFIED**):

- The factor structure was found in *retrospective online reports* of experiences that happened,
  on average, about eight years earlier (MacLean et al. 2012).
- Laboratory fit is marginal: CFI 0.906, just above the conventional 0.90 (Barrett et al. 2015).
- Compared with the MEQ43, the MEQ30 shows "a slight bias toward scoring more observations as
  mystical experiences" (14 false positives in 184).
- Paradoxicality, one of Stace's core features, was dropped.
- Four distinct Stace features collapse into one factor; the authors say this may be specific to
  their sample.
- A stand-alone MEQ30 "has not yet been explicitly validated as a stand-alone instrument."

From critics:

- **The items presuppose metaphysics.** Taves (2020) argues that the evidence for a distinct
  "mystical experience" rests on "the operationalization of a metaphysically untestable
  construct" in the M-scale and the MEQ, and calls for measures that separate what was
  experienced from how the person appraised it. The noetic item ("encountered ultimate
  reality") is the clearest example. **VERIFIED** (abstract)
- **Perennialist and Christian bias.** Mosurinjohn, Roseman & Girn (2023) argue that the
  construct "fail[s] to historicize the concept" and so misses its "perennialist and
  specifically Christian bias." **VERIFIED** (abstract)
- **Self-loss items are not specific.** In the original Pahnke questionnaire, Doblin (1991) found
  that items about loss of self "can be rated highly without having anything to do with mystical
  experiences" (his example: a subject who temporarily forgot his own name). **VERIFIED**
- **Language and culture.** English versions score higher than German ones (Stocker et al. 2026).
  Translations into Brazilian Portuguese, Dutch and Danish reproduce the four factors. No formal
  test of measurement invariance across religions or cultures was found. **VERIFIED** /
  absence of evidence
- **Constructivism.** Katz (1978) holds that "There are NO pure (i.e. unmediated)
  experiences" (p. 26, as quoted in secondary sources): a tradition shapes the experience, not
  only its later description. If so, a
  single cross-traditional scale measures a partly Christian-Protestant template.
  **PARTLY** (secondary sources)

## 5. Why this matters for the Chabad comparison

Three features of the MEQ30 shape everything on [page 4](4-what-chassidus-adds.md):

1. **It measures intensity.** Every item asks *how much*. Nothing asks how much the self was felt
   as the one having the experience.
2. **It is a snapshot.** It is completed once, soon after the session. What happens to the state
   afterwards is left to separate questionnaires.
3. **It sums unity.** Internal and external unity are added together, and the noetic and sacred
   items join them, in one factor.

Chabad sources treat each of those three as the place where the important distinctions live.
