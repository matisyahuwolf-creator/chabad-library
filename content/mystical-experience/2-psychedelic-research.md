# Psychedelics and mystical experience: the evidence

What the studies found, with the numbers, and where the evidence is weaker than it is often
presented. Tags: **VERIFIED** = abstract or full text read; **PARTLY** = some content from a
secondary source. References in full: [bibliography](bibliography.md).

---

## 1. The Good Friday Experiment (1962) and what went wrong with it

On Good Friday 1962, at Marsh Chapel, Boston University, Walter Pahnke gave 20 Protestant
divinity students either 30 mg psilocybin or an active placebo (nicotinic acid) during a
service. He reported: "Four of the ten psilocybin subjects reached the 60 to 70 percent level
of completeness, whereas none of the controls did."

Rick Doblin's follow-up (1991) found 19 of the 20 men and interviewed 16 of them, 24–27 years
later. The psilocybin group still scored higher in every category. But he also reported
**VERIFIED**:

- **The blind failed.** "The double-blind was broken during the service"; by the end of the
  day every subject knew his condition. "The loss of the double-blind makes it impossible to
  determine the relative contributions of psilocybin and suggestion."
- **An adverse event went unreported.** "Pahnke failed to report the administration of the
  tranquilizer thorazine to one of the subjects who received psilocybin," and
  "underemphasized the difficult psychological struggles experienced by most of the psilocybin
  subjects."
- All raw data had been lost.

The founding study of the field had both of the problems that still dog it: broken blinding and
under-reported difficulty.

## 2. The Johns Hopkins series in healthy volunteers

| Study | Design | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Griffiths et al. 2006** | Double-blind crossover, 36 hallucinogen-naïve adults with regular spiritual practice; psilocybin 30 mg/70 kg vs methylphenidate | Complete mystical experience: **22 of 36 (61%)** on psilocybin vs **4 of 36** on methylphenidate. At 2 months, 67% rated it among the five most meaningful experiences of their lives. Also: **31% had significant fear; 17% transient ideas of reference or paranoia.** **VERIFIED** |
| **Griffiths et al. 2008** | 14-month follow-up of the same 36 | 58% still rated it among their five most meaningful experiences, 67% among their five most spiritually significant; mysticism scores correlated with meaning (r = .65) and significance (r = .77), holding after controlling for drug intensity. The title says "mediate"; the analysis was correlation and regression. **VERIFIED** |
| **Griffiths et al. 2011** | Dose-response, N = 18, 0–30 mg/70 kg | At 20–30 mg/70 kg, **72% had a mystical-type experience and 39% had extreme anxiety or fear.** Effects were still present at 14 months. **VERIFIED** |
| **Griffiths et al. 2018** | RCT, 75 people in three arms (very low dose; high dose + standard support; high dose + high support for meditation and spiritual practice) | At 6 months both high-dose arms improved on gratitude, life meaning, forgiveness, closeness, daily spiritual experience, faith and observer ratings. **The determinants were mystical-type experience *and* the amount of meditation or spiritual practice afterwards.** **VERIFIED** |

The last row matters most for this framework: the drug experience predicted lasting change
*together with* continued practice. That is the closest the literature comes to the Chabad
claim that the return matters as much as the ascent ([page 4, §5](4-what-chassidus-adds.md)).

## 3. Clinical trials: does the MEQ score predict who gets better?

| Condition | Study | Result | What the MEQ did |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cancer-related distress | **Griffiths et al. 2016** (N = 51, crossover RCT) | ~80% sustained improvement in depression and anxiety at 6 months | "Mystical-type psilocybin experience on session day mediated the effect of psilocybin dose on therapeutic outcomes." **VERIFIED** |
| Cancer-related distress | **Ross et al. 2016** (N = 29, crossover RCT vs niacin) | ~60–80% sustained reductions at 6.5 months | "The psilocybin-induced mystical experience mediated the therapeutic effect." **VERIFIED** |
| Tobacco | **Johnson et al. 2014; 2017** (N = 15, open label) | 80% abstinent at 6 months; 60% at ~30 months | Abstainers had higher mystical-experience scores than those who kept smoking; overall drug intensity did not differ (Garcia-Romeu et al. 2014). **VERIFIED** |
| Alcohol | **Bogenschutz et al. 2022** (N = 95 RCT vs diphenhydramine) | Heavy-drinking days 9.7% vs 23.6% | MEQ was used to guide dose increases but **not analysed as a predictor.** Blinding: participants guessed correctly in **93.6–94.7%** of sessions. **VERIFIED** |
| Depression | **Davis et al. 2021** (N = 24 completers, waiting-list RCT) | Cohen's d = 2.5–2.6 | The discussion links mystical-type experience to improvement; the statistics are in a supplement not checked here. **PARTLY** |
| Depression | **Carhart-Harris et al. 2021** (N = 59, vs escitalopram) | Primary outcome not significant (difference 2.0 points, p = .17) | Secondary analyses: MEQ and emotional breakthrough predicted improvement (Murphy et al. 2022); mystical experience and ego dissolution uniquely mediated the psilocybin effect (Weiss et al. 2024). **VERIFIED** (abstracts) |
| Treatment-resistant depression | **Goodwin et al. 2022** (N = 233, COMP360) | 25 mg vs 1 mg: −6.6 MADRS points at week 3 | **Did not use the MEQ.** Later analysis: emotional breakthrough (r = −.64) and oceanic boundlessness (r = −.51) correlated with improvement (Goodwin et al. 2025). **VERIFIED** |

### Pooled evidence

- **Ko et al. 2022**, systematic review of 12 studies: 10 found a significant link between
  mystical experience and outcome; 6 of the 12 were open-label; no pooled estimate. **VERIFIED**
- **Romeo et al. 2025**, meta-analysis: **r = .33** between mystical-experience intensity and
  clinical improvement. Stronger in mood disorders (.41) than addiction (.19), in clinical
  protocols (.50) than naturalistic use (.14), and in prospective (.43) than retrospective (.14)
  designs. **VERIFIED** (abstract)
- **Deutsch et al. 2026**, scoping review of 13 trials (n = 410): 69% reported a positive
  MEQ–outcome relationship (80% in life-threatening illness, 63% in depression). **VERIFIED**
- **Kugel et al. 2025**, review of 98 studies: *insight* was associated with improvement in 25 of
  29 studies, an association "often stronger than mystical-type experience." **VERIFIED**

**Reading it plainly.** A moderate, fairly consistent correlation. Correlation is not mechanism,
and in the trials the drug group both had the experiences and knew it had the drug.

## 4. Religion, God, and belief

- **"God encounter" survey** (Griffiths et al. 2019): 4,285 people, comparing non-drug encounters
  (n = 809) with psilocybin, LSD, ayahuasca and DMT. Complete mystical experience on the MEQ30:
  **64% psychedelic vs 43% non-drug.** What was encountered: "God" 41% non-drug vs 18%
  psychedelic; "Ultimate Reality" 55% psychedelic vs 26% non-drug. Self-identified atheists fell
  from 21% to 8% in the psychedelic group. Retrospective, self-selected. **VERIFIED**
- **Clergy study** (Griffiths et al. 2025, Johns Hopkins and NYU): religious leaders from several
  traditions, randomised to immediate or delayed psilocybin (n = 13 and 16; 24 completed both
  sessions). At 16 months, 96% rated a session among their five most spiritually significant
  experiences and 42% as the single most profound experience of their lives; 46% rated a session
  among their five most psychologically challenging. No serious adverse events. Rabbis took part:
  a participating rabbi described "four other rabbis, a Catholic priest, several Protestant
  ministers and a Muslim leader," and the sample was reported as 76% Christian (Religion News
  Service; the paper's own demographic table was not checked). **The paper prints an IRB-audit
  disclosure** of unapproved team members, a funder on the study team, and conflicts "not
  appropriately disclosed nor managed." **VERIFIED** (abstract and published excerpt) /
  **PARTLY** (tradition breakdown)
- **Zen retreat** (Smigielski et al. 2019): 39 experienced meditators at a five-day retreat,
  psilocybin vs placebo. "Strong" mystical experience on the Hood scale: **19 of 20 on psilocybin
  vs 3 of 19 on placebo.** Loss of ego boundaries predicted change at 4 months. Used the M-scale
  and 5D-ASC, not the MEQ. **VERIFIED**
- **Metaphysical beliefs** (Timmermann et al. 2021): after psychedelic use, beliefs moved away from
  physicalism (d = .33 at 4 weeks, .46 at 6 months) and toward panpsychism. **VERIFIED**

## 5. The mechanism debate

- **Yaden & Griffiths (2021):** with overall intensity controlled, specific subjective effects
  still predict outcome, so the experience is "necessary" and accounts for "the majority of
  their benefit." **VERIFIED**
- **Olson (2021):** the evidence is "largely correlational"; drug-induced neural plasticity might
  be enough without the experience. **VERIFIED**

The debate is open. It matters for Chabad's contribution because Chassidus is entirely on the
"the experience and what one does with it are the point" side, but with a different account of
*which* features of the experience count.

## 6. Critiques

- **Expectancy and broken blinding.** A systematic review found trials rarely report
  expectations or whether blinding held; effect sizes "are likely over-estimated"
  (Muthukumaraswamy, Forsyth & Lumley 2021). **VERIFIED**
- **Placebo can produce psychedelic-like states.** In a party-like setting with actors, some of
  33 placebo participants reached altered-state scores "typically associated with moderate or high
  doses of psilocybin" (Olson et al. 2020). This was measured with the 5D-ASC, **not** the MEQ.
  In controlled trials, 4 of 36 met the MEQ "complete" criterion after methylphenidate (Griffiths
  2006) and 3 of 19 placebo meditators met the Hood-scale criterion (Smigielski 2019).
  **VERIFIED**
- **False insights.** Psychedelics may raise "the quantity and subjective intensity of insights
  and thence beliefs, including false ones" (McGovern et al. 2024). **VERIFIED** This is the modern
  form of the Mitteler Rebbe's warning about a counterfeit that "mixes falsehood and truth
  together" ([page 4, §7](4-what-chassidus-adds.md)).
- **Move past mysticism?** Sanders & Zijlmans (2021) argue the field should drop a framework tied to
  "supernatural or nonempirical belief systems." Breeksema & van Elk (2021) reply that the
  "weirdness" should stay central. **VERIFIED**
- **The construct itself.** See [page 1, §4](1-the-meq.md) (Taves 2020; Mosurinjohn et al. 2023;
  Katz 1978).
- **Harms are real at high doses.** 31% significant fear (2006); 39% extreme anxiety or fear at
  20–30 mg/70 kg (2011); 46% of clergy rated a session among their most challenging (2025).

## 7. Where things stand (as of 8 October 2026)

- **Psilocybin** has no FDA approval and remains Schedule I. **PARTLY**: no approval found up to
  early October 2026.
- **COMP360 (Compass Pathways)** passed two phase 3 trials in treatment-resistant depression:
  COMP005, single 25 mg vs placebo, −3.6 MADRS points at week 6 (June 2025); COMP006, two 25 mg
  doses vs 1 mg, −3.8 at week 6 (February 2026). The company said in August 2026 it was on track
  to complete its rolling application in Q4 2026; as of late September it was not complete.
  **VERIFIED** (company filings)
- **MDMA-assisted therapy** was rejected by the FDA on 9 August 2024. Trade press reported a
  resubmission without a new phase 3 trial in August 2026; no FDA acceptance was found.
  **PARTLY**
- **US Executive Order 14401** (18 April 2026) directs faster FDA review for breakthrough
  psychedelic drugs and DEA review of rescheduling once a product is approved. **VERIFIED**
  (Federal Register)
- **Australia** has allowed authorised psychiatrists to prescribe psilocybin for treatment-resistant
  depression since 1 July 2023. **VERIFIED**
- **Oregon** (licensed psilocybin services since 2023) and **Colorado** (first healing-centre licence
  in March 2025) run state programs; current numbers were not confirmed. **PARTLY**
