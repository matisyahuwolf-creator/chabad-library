# Beyond the drug: contemplation, prayer, and the Jewish story

Mystical-type experience is not a drug effect only. This page collects what happens when the
same questionnaires are given to people who meditate or pray, what predicts who has such
experiences at all, what goes wrong in contemplative practice, and the history of Jewish and
Chabad encounters with psychedelics. Tags as before: **VERIFIED**, **PARTLY**, **UNVERIFIED**.
References: [bibliography](bibliography.md).

---

## 1. The same scales, no drug

Scales, recall windows and scoring differ between these studies; most are **not** head-to-head
comparisons.

| Setting | N | Result | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spontaneous "God encounter" (survey; Griffiths et al. 2019) | 809 non-drug vs 1,184 psilocybin | "Complete" on the MEQ30: **43%** non-drug vs **62%** psilocybin | VERIFIED |
| Experiences that changed attitudes to death (survey; Sweeney et al. 2022) | 933 non-drug vs 766 psilocybin | MEQ30 total .68 vs .74 of maximum; complete 47% vs 51%. Non-drug participants were *more* likely to call it the single most meaningful experience of their lives | VERIFIED |
| Christian Centering Prayer, online RCT (Rohde, …, Yaden 2024) | 702 | Religiously framed prayer raised MEQ scores over a passive control (d = 0.37) but did **not** differ significantly from the same practice framed in secular terms | VERIFIED |
| Month-long insight-meditation retreat vs controls (Zanesco et al. 2023), Hood scale | 32 | Retreatants higher on internal unity, transcendence, sacredness, noetic quality, positive affect | VERIFIED |
| Fire Kasina meditation retreat (Woollacott et al. 2024) | 6 | MEQ 85% of maximum, above moderate-dose psilocybin | VERIFIED (pilot) |
| Circular breathwork (Havenith et al. 2025) | 61 | MEQ30 subscales "statistically indistinguishable" from 20–25 mg psilocybin | VERIFIED (exploratory) |
| Hypnosis (Corlett et al. 2025) | 10 | 70% "complete" | VERIFIED (small) |
| Group virtual reality (Glowacki et al. 2022) | 58 | MEQ30 "statistically indistinguishable" from published psychedelic studies | VERIFIED |
| "Gateway Experience" audio program | 12 | No effect on MEQ30 | VERIFIED |

**The closest analogue to hisbonenus** is the Centering Prayer trial: a theistic contemplative
practice, measured with the MEQ, in a randomised design. Its finding that religious framing did
not beat secular framing on the MEQ is worth keeping in mind: the MEQ may not see what is
religiously specific about a practice.

### Where contemplative and psychedelic reports differ

- In narrative reports, **mystical-type insights were *more* frequent after meditation** (n = 66)
  than after psychedelics (n = 147); value insights were more frequent after psychedelics
  (Jylkkä et al. 2025). **VERIFIED**
- A language analysis of 197 reports found highly similar content, but **psychedelic reports were
  more emotionally charged, positively and negatively** (Kallio-Mannila et al. 2025). **VERIFIED**
  This is *consistent with*, not proof of, the Chabad claim that contemplative depth is quieter
  than intensity ([page 4, §2](4-what-chassidus-adds.md)).
- Meditation and psychedelics both disrupt self-consciousness, but narrative self-loss and bodily
  self-loss are different, and a transient loss is not a trait change (Millière et al. 2018).
  **VERIFIED**
- A study of 386 people found **16 distinct characteristics** of "loss of self" across meditation,
  psychedelic and pathological contexts (Canby et al. 2024): self-loss is not one thing.
  **VERIFIED** That is the opening for Chabad's distinctions between kinds of *bittul*.

## 2. Who has these experiences: absorption, expectancy, framing

- **Absorption** (Tellegen's trait of openness to absorbing, self-altering experience) was the
  strongest non-drug predictor of pleasant and mystical-type responses across 409 psilocybin
  administrations (Studerus et al. 2012), and predicted mystical, challenging and visual
  experience prospectively (Haijen et al. 2018). **VERIFIED**
- High-absorption people report hearing God in prayer and "intense mystical experiences in
  response to psychedelics or placebo brain-stimulation" (Lifshitz, van Elk & Luhrmann 2019).
  Absorption plus practice predicted vivid prayer experience among evangelicals (Luhrmann,
  Nusbaum & Thisted 2010) and, with a culture's view of the mind as "porous," felt presence across
  five countries (Luhrmann et al. 2021). **VERIFIED**
- The "God helmet": with a sham field, sensed presence and mystical experience were predicted by
  suggestibility and absorption, not by the magnetic field (Granqvist et al. 2005, N = 89,
  double-blind). **VERIFIED**
- **Religious set matters on drugs too:** religious identification and religious intent predicted
  higher Mysticism Scale scores on psychedelics (Neitzke-Spruill & Glasser 2018, N = 119).
  **VERIFIED**

The Tanya already assumes individual differences: «כָּל חַד לְפוּם שִׁיעוּרָא דִילֵיהּ [יֵשׁ מִתְפָּעֵל כוּ׳ וְיֵשׁ מִתְפָּעֵל כוּ׳]»,
*each according to his measure; one is moved this way, another that* (Tanya 50:5). A study that
does not measure absorption cannot tell a practice's effect from its practitioners' talent.

## 3. When contemplation goes wrong

- **Varieties of Contemplative Experience** (Lindahl et al. 2017): interviews with 60 Buddhist
  practitioners and 32 teachers yielded **59 kinds of experience in 7 domains**, including fear,
  anxiety, panic or paranoia (82%), with a median duration of 1–3 years. The sample was chosen
  for difficulties, so these are not prevalence rates. **VERIFIED**
- In mindfulness-based cognitive therapy, 83% had at least one meditation-related effect and
  **6–14% had lasting bad effects** (Britton et al. 2021). **VERIFIED**
- Among 1,232 regular meditators, 25.6% reported particularly unpleasant experiences; **religious
  participants were less likely to**, and purely deconstructive practice and retreats raised the
  risk (Schlosser et al. 2019). **VERIFIED**
- Researchers now propose meditation's adverse effects as a comparison set for psychedelic ones
  (Palitsky et al. 2024). **VERIFIED**

Chabad sources have their own map of what goes wrong: the counterfeit arousal, the dulled heart,
ascent without return. [Page 4](4-what-chassidus-adds.md) sets those out. Schlosser's finding
that a religious frame was protective is one reason to study a contemplative practice that comes
inside a full religious life.

## 4. Jewish practice: the gap

- **No published study has given the MEQ30 or MEQ43 to Jews in prayer, hisbonenus or Kabbalistic
  meditation**, and no peer-reviewed brain-imaging study of Orthodox or Hasidic prayer was found.
- The only Hood-scale study of religious Jews found is Lazar & Kravetz (2005); only its citation
  could be confirmed. **PARTLY**
- Lazar (2020) argues that measures built on Christian samples transfer poorly to Israeli Jewish
  samples, and that Jewish religiosity is more a matter of practice (orthopraxy) than of belief
  (orthodoxy). **VERIFIED** (abstract)
- Arzy & Idel, *Kabbalah: A Neurocognitive Approach to Mystical Experiences* (Yale, 2015), reads
  Kabbalistic first-person texts neurologically; it does not study living practitioners.
  **PARTLY**
- A Chabad-descended precursor: **Fischl Schneersohn** (1887–1958) recast Hasidic ecstasy as a
  psychotherapy, his "science of man" (Freis 2020). **VERIFIED**

## 5. Theory: hisbonenus is a different kind of practice

- The leading psychedelic models (the "entropic brain," Carhart-Harris et al. 2014, 2018; REBUS,
  Carhart-Harris & Friston 2019) say psychedelics relax the grip of high-level beliefs. REBUS
  explicitly extends this to "overly-confident political, religious, and/or philosophical
  perspectives." **VERIFIED**
- The matching meditation model (Laukkonen & Slagter 2021) describes **deconstructive** practice
  that progressively drops conceptual thought. **VERIFIED**
- Dahl, Lutz & Davidson (2015) sort meditation into **attentional, constructive and
  deconstructive** families. **VERIFIED**

**Ours.** *Hisbonenus* is effortful, detailed, conceptual contemplation that aims at *bittul*.
It sits mostly in the **constructive** family, with a deconstructive end-state. Both main models
are built on loosening; hisbonenus builds. That is the Tohu/Tikkun point of
[page 4, §4](4-what-chassidus-adds.md) in neuroscientific dress: one approach loosens the vessels,
the other enlarges them. It also means findings from deconstructive meditation may not transfer.

## 6. Chabad and psychedelics: the history

### The Rebbe on LSD (1964) and drugs (1972)

A letter of 20 Marcheshvan 5725 (26 October 1964), answering a young man who asked on behalf of
friends with a yeshiva background whether LSD could bring spiritual heights. **VERIFIED**
(chabad.org)

- "Biochemistry is not my field, and I cannot express an opinion on the drug you mention,
  especially as it is still new."
- Even granting such effects, it "is not the proper way to attain mystical inspiration."
- "The Jewish way is to go from strength to strength," using one's natural capacities: drugs
  "have a place only if they are necessary for the physical health," and "before any drugs are
  taken one should first utilize all one's natural capacities."
- He then prescribes fixed times for Torah and Chassidus, prayer, tefillin and kashrus, and that
  the essential thing is the deed.

(A separate chabad.org page dates the letter to 1965; the date printed on the letter is 1964.)

In 1972 the Rebbe spoke of drugs as spreading among youth:
«שענין ה"סמים" (כפי שנקרא כאן: "דראָגס") הולך ומתפשט» (Toras Menachem vol. 70 [5733, part 1],
Motza'ei Shabbos Vayishlach, 19 Kislev, §39). **VERIFIED** (corpus) No 1960s sicha on youth turning
to drugs for spiritual experience was found.

### The Rebbe's call for "kosher" therapeutic meditation (1977–1979)

- **Memo, Teves 5738 (winter 1977–78)**, for "Rabbis, doctors, and laymen": Transcendental
  Meditation, yoga and guru movements border on idolatry; enlist neurologists and psychiatrists
  to perfect non-idolatrous methods and publicise them. **PARTLY** (excerpts only)
- **Letters, 21 Adar II and 11 Sivan 5738 (March and June 1978)**: the institute should have **no
  religious preconditions** for clients; "start immediately a pilot clinic"; send a budget; and
  "I do not think it advisable to use the term 'mystic'." **VERIFIED** (chabad.org, *Healthy in
  Body, Mind & Spirit* vol. 3, ch. 9)
- **Public talk, 13 Tammuz 5739 (8 July 1979)**: physicians treating emotional disorders "have a
  sacred obligation to find a way to utilize the therapeutic benefits of meditation in a Kosher
  way." **VERIFIED** (chabad.org)
- The psychologist **Dr. Yehuda Landes** is reported to have led the effort; a 1979 letter to
  him reportedly says "Your function is that of physicians (not Torah teachers)." **PARTLY**
  (conference handout; the handout itself contains date errors)

**Ours.** The Rebbe asked for exactly what [page 5](5-research-program.md) proposes in a narrower
form: physicians, measurement, a pilot, publication. He also kept the therapeutic project
separate from the mystical one, which a study design should respect.

### Reb Zalman Schachter-Shalomi

- Ordained at Tomchei Temimim in 1947. **VERIFIED** (University of Colorado archive)
- First LSD experience with Timothy Leary: **1962** according to the archive's timeline, **summer
  1963** according to Sam Shonkoff, citing an archive typescript. **Unresolved.**
- His 1966 lecture "Kabbalah and LSD" led to a parting of ways with Chabad's leadership.
  **VERIFIED** (archive's account)
- In *Commentary*, August 1966: "The psychedelic experience can be not only a challenge but also a
  support of my faith. After seeing what really happens at the point where all is One… I can also
  see Judaism in a new and amazing light." **VERIFIED**
- The story that the Rebbe blessed him before taking LSD is **contested**; accounts on both sides
  are documented, the primary documents are not.

### Since then

- "Itzik Lodzer" (Arthur Green), "Notes from the Jewish Underground: Psychedelics and Kabbalah,"
  *Response*, 1968. **PARTLY**
- **Shefa**, Jewish psychedelic support, founded by Rabbi Zac Kamenetz; the first **Jewish
  Psychedelic Summit**, online, 2–3 May 2021. **VERIFIED**
- The 2025 **clergy psilocybin study** included five rabbis, one Orthodox (Kamenetz) per press
  reports; no Chabad rabbi is mentioned. **PARTLY** (see [page 2, §4](2-psychedelic-research.md))
- An Emory/Shefa survey of Jewish psychedelic experience launched in November 2024; no results
  were found. **VERIFIED** (launch)
- **Halacha:** no published Orthodox ruling on psilocybin- or MDMA-assisted therapy was found.
- **Israel:** two of the MAPS phase 3 MDMA trials had Israeli sites (Mitchell et al. 2021, 2023);
  studies of Nova festival survivors who were on psychedelics during the 7 October 2023 attack
  (Simon et al. 2025, 2026); ayahuasca groups of Israelis and Palestinians (Roseman et al. 2021).
  **VERIFIED** No Israeli study recruiting Orthodox, Haredi or Hasidic participants was found.

## 7. What makes change last: integration

- Openness stayed above baseline for over a year in those who had a mystical experience
  (MacLean, Johnson & Griffiths 2011). **VERIFIED**
- Mystical-type experience **and continued meditation or spiritual practice** together predicted
  lasting change (Griffiths et al. 2018). **VERIFIED**
- **Communitas**, the felt togetherness of a group, correlated with gains in well-being (r = .22)
  and social connectedness (r = .25) (Kettner et al. 2021, N = 886). **VERIFIED** The Chabad
  *farbrengen* is a standing institution of exactly this kind ([page 4, §8](4-what-chassidus-adds.md)).
- Set and rapport explained 40% of the variance in mystical experience in a small controlled
  sample (Angyus et al. 2024). **VERIFIED**
- "Integration" now has a formal concept analysis (Bathje, Majeski & Kudowor 2022). **VERIFIED**
- Nova survivors' experiences "gained therapeutic meaning when held within supportive, culturally
  resonant contexts" (Simon et al. 2025). **VERIFIED**
