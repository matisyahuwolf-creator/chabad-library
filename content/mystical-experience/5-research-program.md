# A research program: testing what Chassidus claims

[Page 4](4-what-chassidus-adds.md) turned Chabad texts into claims about mystical experience.
This page turns those claims into things a study can measure, with hypotheses that could come
out wrong. The MEQ30 stays as the common currency, so results can be compared with the
psychedelic literature; the Chassidus-derived measures sit beside it, not in place of it.

**Status of everything on this page: Ours, untested.** The draft items below are proposals.
None has been piloted, translated, or validated.

**A precedent.** In 1978 the Lubavitcher Rebbe urged physicians to "start immediately a pilot
clinic" for a non-idolatrous therapeutic meditation, open to all, and advised against the word
"mystic" ([page 3, §6](3-beyond-the-drug.md)). A measured, published study of Chabad
contemplation is in the line of that request, so long as the therapeutic and the devotional
are kept distinct.

## 1. Seven constructs the MEQ30 does not capture

| # | Construct | Source (page 4) | What it asks | Draft way to measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Felt self in the peak** | Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 9; Pirush HaMilos; Ayin Beis on Tohu | Was the self felt *as the one having* the experience, including feeling oneself dissolve? | 4–6 items rated 0–5, e.g. "I was strongly aware of myself as the one going through this"; "My own dissolving felt like a dramatic event happening to me" (reverse-keyed partner: "There was no 'me' noticing it") |
| C2 | **Direction: exit or return** | Ratzo v'shov; Likkutei Sichos 37 Tazria; Ayin Beis §613 | Did the experience pull toward leaving the body and casting life aside, or toward re-ordering life? | Paired items: "I wanted to leave my body/this world behind" vs "I felt drawn back toward my body, family and obligations with new purpose" |
| C3 | **Specificity ("an accounting")** | Ayin Beis §613:2 | Was it tied to particulars of the person's own life, or only global? | "The experience showed me specific things in my own conduct to change" (0–5) plus a count of specifics named in a free-text answer |
| C4 | **Constancy** | Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 14:12 | Afterwards, does the state hold, or fall and need re-kindling? | Daily or weekly diary (EMA) for 4–8 weeks: presence of the core state, and effort needed to recall it |
| C5 | **Generativity** | Kuntres HaHitpa'alut 21:11–14 | Does understanding keep opening, or shrink to an outline? | Follow-up interview at 1 and 3 months, coded for new derived insights vs repetition of the original description |
| C6 | **Vessel** (capacity to hold it) | Tohu and Tikkun; the Rebbe on "broad vessels" | Was there a framework, training and steadiness to hold the experience? | Prior contemplative hours; a validated psychological-flexibility scale; depth of a meaning framework for the experience; trait absorption (Tellegen) measured separately, since it is a talent, not a vessel |
| C7 | **Object of the arousal** | Ayin Beis, part 1, §66:4 | Was the person moved by "the good of the thing itself" or by "how good it is for me"? | Appraisal items: self-referential ("This was wonderful *for me*") vs object-focused ("What I saw was good in itself") |

## 2. Hypotheses

Each hypothesis is stated so that a plain result could refute it.

- **H1 (felt self).** Among people with the same MEQ30 total, higher C1 during the peak predicts
  *less* persisting positive change at 1–6 months. *Refuted if* C1 adds nothing beyond MEQ30, or
  predicts in the opposite direction.
- **H2 (direction).** A "return" orientation (C2) predicts benefit; an "exit" orientation
  predicts adverse outcomes (depersonalisation, derealisation, withdrawal from obligations).
  *Refuted if* direction is unrelated to outcome once MEQ30 and challenging-experience scores
  are controlled.
- **H3 (vessel).** The link between MEQ30 score and lasting benefit is stronger where C6 is
  higher. *Refuted if* there is no moderation.
- **H4 (persistence signs).** Constancy (C4) and generativity (C5) at one month predict
  six-month outcomes over and above MEQ30 and the Persisting Effects Questionnaire at one month.
- **H5 (two unities).** Internal-unity and external-unity items predict outcomes differently,
  so summing them into one factor loses information. *Exploratory.*
- **H6 (object).** Object-focused appraisal (C7) predicts benefit better than self-referential
  appraisal at equal intensity. *Exploratory.*

## 3. Four study designs, from cheapest to hardest

1. **Retrospective survey (feasible now).** Practitioners of Chabad *hisbonenus* and Chassidic
   prayer rate their most significant contemplative experience on the MEQ30 plus C1–C3 and C7.
   A second arm: people who have had both a contemplative and a psychedelic experience rate
   each, as in the within-person design of the "God encounter" survey (Griffiths et al. 2019;
   see [page 2](2-psychedelic-research.md)). Compare profiles, not just totals.
2. **Prospective contemplative study.** An 8-week course of structured *hisbonenus*, following
   the order of Kuntres HaTefillah (preparation, detailed contemplation, prayer). MEQ30 after
   selected sessions; C4 by diary; C5 by interview; outcomes at 3 and 6 months.
3. **Add-on module to licensed psilocybin trials.** C1–C3 and C7 added after the session,
   C4–C5 at follow-up. Tests incremental validity over MEQ30 in the setting where the MEQ30's
   predictive claims were made. No change to the drug protocol.
4. **Phenomenological interviews.** Micro-phenomenological or similar interviews of both
   groups, coded blind with the Chabad distinctions (felt/unfelt, run/return, general/specific,
   for-me/in-itself). Inter-rater agreement is itself a result: it shows whether the
   distinctions are observable at all.

## 4. What would count against the Chabad claims

- C1–C7 add no prediction beyond the MEQ30 and existing scales.
- Coders cannot reliably tell "felt" from "unfelt" self in interviews.
- Contemplative and psychedelic profiles on the MEQ30 are indistinguishable *and* their
  outcomes follow the same MEQ30 slope.
- The persistence signs (C4, C5) are just later readings of the same intensity.

Any of these would be a real finding and should be published as one.

## 5. Measurement cautions

- **The MEQ30 was built and validated with psilocybin.** Its factor structure in
  non-drug, and especially in Jewish contemplative, samples has to be checked before scores
  are compared. A Hebrew or Yiddish version would need forward-back translation and
  invariance testing. *We have not confirmed whether a validated Hebrew MEQ30 exists.*
- **Expectancy.** Telling people what they will feel shapes what they report. Placebo alone
  produced psilocybin-like altered states in some participants (Olson et al. 2020, measured
  with the 5D-ASC, not the MEQ; see [page 2](2-psychedelic-research.md)). Do not name the
  target states before the experience.
- **Absorption** predicts mystical-type reports in drug, placebo and prayer contexts alike
  ([page 3](3-beyond-the-drug.md)). Measure it and control for it.
- **Framing.** In a Centering Prayer RCT, religious framing did not raise MEQ scores over secular
  framing (Rohde et al. 2024). If the MEQ cannot see what is religiously specific, that is a
  reason for the added constructs, and a reason not to expect group differences on the MEQ alone.
- **Hisbonenus is constructive, not deconstructive** (Dahl, Lutz & Davidson 2015 typology). Do
  not borrow effect sizes or adverse-effect rates from deconstructive meditation without saying so.
- **Retrospective ratings** of a peak drift with time and with the person's later framework.
  Rate as close to the event as possible.
- **Chabad terms are not diagnoses.** "Felt self" and "exit orientation" are research
  constructs drawn from the texts, not judgements of a person's spiritual standing.

## 6. Ethics, safety and halacha

- Nothing here proposes giving any substance outside a licensed, ethically approved trial, and
  nothing on this site is advice to take one.
- Screen for personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder in any arm that
  intensifies experience, drug or contemplative. Meditation has its own adverse effects
  ([page 3](3-beyond-the-drug.md)).
- Religious participants should be told plainly what is being studied. Questions of halacha
  (for example, about psychedelic therapy) belong to the participant's own rabbinic authority,
  not to the research team.
- Do not market Chassidus as a "drug-free psychedelic." The texts make a different claim: that
  the measure of an experience is the person it leaves behind.
