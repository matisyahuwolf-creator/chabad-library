# Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn — the "Tzemach Tzedek"

**English name:** Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn
**Hebrew name:** רבי מנחם מענדל שניאורסאהן
**Honorific / title:** The *Tzemach Tzedek* (צמח צדק — "Righteous Sprout/Offspring"), third Rebbe of Chabad-Lubavitch; commonly called "the Tzemach Tzedek" after his halachic magnum opus
**Life years:** 1789–1866 (29 Elul 5549 – 13 Nissan 5626)
**Years of leadership:** c. 1827/1828–1866 (some accounts give a formal assumption of leadership in 1831, after a multi-year interregnum, until his own passing)

The Tzemach Tzedek was the third Rebbe of the Chabad-Lubavitch dynasty and a towering figure who stood at the confluence of three lineages. He was the **grandson of Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi** (the Alter Rebbe, founder of Chabad — whose daughter Devorah Leah was his mother), and the **son-in-law, nephew, and successor of Rabbi DovBer Schneuri** (the Mitteler Rebbe, the second Rebbe). Because the Mitteler Rebbe was a son of the Alter Rebbe and a brother of Devorah Leah, he was simultaneously the Tzemach Tzedek's **maternal uncle**; his wife Chaya Mushka was therefore his **first cousin**. Orphaned of his mother in infancy, he was raised and educated by his grandfather the Alter Rebbe, becoming his foremost disciple.

The Mitteler Rebbe passed away on 9 Kislev 5588 (December 1827). The Tzemach Tzedek accepted the leadership only with great reluctance — Chabad sources record that he assumed it c. 1827–1828 (some give a formal date of 3 Sivan 5588 / 1828), while other accounts date his formal assumption to the eve of Shavuot 5591 (May 1831), after a multi-year interregnum during which he repeatedly sought to have others lead. (Note: the framing that leadership began "from the passing of his father-in-law in 1831" is incorrect — the Mitteler Rebbe died in 1827, not 1831.) Upon the Tzemach Tzedek's own passing in 1866 the leadership ultimately passed to his youngest son, **Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn (the Rebbe Maharash)**, the fourth Rebbe, while several of his other sons established their own Chassidic courts (Kopust, Liadi, Niezhin, Avrutch, Bobruisk). His defining contribution to Chabad literature is twofold and almost without parallel: he was simultaneously a first-rank *posek* (halachic decisor), whose responsa earned the respect of the entire rabbinic world, and the most prolific author of Chassidic discourses in the dynasty, producing the vast *Or HaTorah* corpus that the Lubavitcher Rebbe described as effectively containing the teachings of all the Chabad Rebbeim before and after him.

## The Corpus at a Glance

The Tzemach Tzedek's output is exceptionally large and is best understood as falling into three distinct streams, an unusual breadth for a single Chassidic master:

1. **Halacha (nigleh) — responsa, legal rulings, and Talmudic novellae**, headed by the *Shut Tzemach Tzedek*, the work whose title became his name. These address more than a thousand practical legal questions across all four sections of the Shulchan Aruch and stand as a recognized classic of 19th-century *halachic* literature.
2. **Chassidus / philosophy — maamarim (discourses) and theoretical works**, the largest stream by far. It centers on *Or HaTorah* (roughly 40+ volumes of discourses on the entire Torah, Tanach, the festivals, the prayers, and rabbinic sayings; published by Kehot in 30-, 35-, and 37-volume sets), the early classic *Derech Mitzvosecha* on the mystical rationales of the commandments, the philosophical *Sefer HaChakira – Derech Emunah*, the Kabbalistic *Biurei HaZohar*, and *Yahel Or* on Psalms.
3. **Letters (igros) and personal/communal writings**, a smaller but historically important body, including roughly 90 surviving non-halachic letters reflecting his leadership in defending Russian Jewry.

Most of the discourse material was not "published" by the author in finished book form; it was transcribed from his manuscripts and from the notes of transcribers (*chozrim/manichim*), and has been edited and released over more than a century — a process Kehot continues to this day. Nearly all of the corpus is in **Rabbinic Hebrew/Aramaic**; only a relatively small portion exists in **English translation**.

## Complete Works

### Halacha — Responsa & Legal Writings

**שו"ת צמח צדק** — *Shut Tzemach Tzedek* ("Responsa of the Tzemach Tzedek")
- **Type:** Halachic responsa, legal rulings (*piskei dinim*), and Talmudic novellae.
- **Language:** Hebrew (with Aramaic).
- **Extent:** Multi-volume, organized by the four sections of the Shulchan Aruch (Orach Chaim, Yoreh De'ah, Even HaEzer, Choshen Mishpat), plus *piskei dinim* and *chiddushim*; more than a thousand responsa. Sold today as a 7-part set in 5 volumes; Kehot offers it bound in various groupings (e.g., a 2-volume Orach Chaim).
- **Date:** Authored across his lifetime; first edition printed posthumously in **Vilna, 1871–1884** (Even HaEzer 1872, Yoreh De'ah 1874, Orach Chaim 1884).
- **Description:** His most famous halachic work — the compendium whose title became his sobriquet (in gematria, *Tzemach* [צמח] = *Menachem* [מנחם], and *Tzedek* [צדק] = *Mendel* [מענדל]); a recognized classic consulted across the rabbinic world.
- **Original online:** Kehot print edition — https://store.kehotonline.com/prodinfo.asp?number=HTZ-TZEMT.SB (paid). Free scans of early editions are available on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org; search "צמח צדק שו״ת") — note that HebrewBooks blocks automated access, so verify specific records in a browser.
- **English:** No full English translation.

**פסקי דינים / חידושים על הש"ס** — *Piskei Dinim / Chiddushim al HaShas* ("Legal Decisions / Novellae on the Talmud")
- **Type:** Glosses, legal decisions, and Talmudic commentary.
- **Language:** Hebrew/Aramaic.
- **Extent:** Several volumes; partly integrated into / appended to the responsa corpus and printed in dedicated volumes.
- **Date:** Lifetime; printed posthumously (late 19th c. onward).
- **Description:** His running halachic decisions on the Shulchan Aruch and novellae on tractates of the Talmud.
- **Original online:** Available within the Kehot responsa/legal set (paid). Free scans likely on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** No full English translation.

**הגהות / רשימות על תורה אור, ליקוטי תורה ותניא** — *Hagahos / Reshimos al Torah Or, Likkutei Torah, v'Tanya* ("Glosses / Notes on Torah Or, Likkutei Torah, and Tanya")
- **Type:** Marginal glosses, annotations, and short notes on the Alter Rebbe's core Chassidic works.
- **Language:** Hebrew.
- **Extent:** Printed as appended glosses within editions of *Likkutei Torah* and *Torah Or*, and as collected *hagahos* on *Tanya*; also drawn into *Or HaTorah*.
- **Date:** Across his lifetime; printed within the Alter Rebbe's works (from the 1848 *Likkutei Torah* onward) and in later Kehot collections.
- **Description:** A recognized, separately-cited part of his corpus: concise clarifications and expansions on his grandfather's discourses, frequently cross-referenced in study of Chabad Chassidus.
- **Original online:** Printed within Kehot/standard editions of *Likkutei Torah*, *Torah Or*, and *Tanya*; *Likkutei Torah* and *Torah Or* (with glosses) are on Sefaria — https://www.sefaria.org/texts/Chasidut (free).
- **English:** No dedicated English edition (these are brief glosses, not a standalone book).

### Chassidus — Maamarim & Theoretical Works

**אור התורה** — *Or HaTorah* ("Light of the Torah")
- **Type:** Chassidic discourses (maamarim) / running commentary.
- **Language:** Hebrew.
- **Extent:** Roughly **40+ volumes** of content; Kehot publishes it in **30-, 35-, and 37-volume sets** (sources commonly cite ~41–42 volumes of content). Covers the Five Books (Bereishis, Shemos, Vayikra, Bamidbar, Devarim), Nach (Prophets & Writings, including the Eichah/Lamentations material), the Megillos including Shir HaShirim and Esther, the festivals (*Moadim*), rabbinic sayings (*Maamarei Razal / Inyanim*), and the prayers/Siddur.
- **Date:** Discourses delivered/written across his life; **first volume (Bereishis, with Chanukah) published 1913 in Berditchev** by R. Chaim Eliezer Bichovski; the Lubavitcher Rebbe restarted and greatly expanded publication through **Kehot from 1950**, continuing under R. Aharon Chitrik and others — still being released.
- **Description:** His central Chassidic work and the largest discourse corpus of any Chabad Rebbe; a vast synthesis weaving the Babylonian and Jerusalem Talmuds, Midrash, Kabbalah, and Chassidus. The Lubavitcher Rebbe said it contains the teachings of all the Chabad Rebbeim.
- **Original online:** Kehot print sets (paid) — e.g. the 37-volume set https://store.kehotonline.com/prodinfo.asp?number=HTZ-OH.S and a 30/35-volume set https://store.kehotonline.com/prodinfo.asp?number=HTZ-OT.S (the differing counts reflect different bindings/printings of the same corpus, not different content). Free early-edition scans are hosted on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — specific record-to-volume mappings are unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** No comprehensive English translation; only selected discourses appear in English anthologies and shiurim.

**דרך מצותיך / טעמי המצות** — *Derech Mitzvosecha* ("The Way of Your Commandments"; subtitle *Taamei HaMitzvos*, "Reasons for the Commandments")
- **Type:** Chassidic/Kabbalistic exposition of the rationales (*taamim*) of the mitzvos; a foundational text of Chabad philosophy. Includes the companion *Perush HaMilos* (commentary on the words of prayer) in some editions.
- **Language:** Hebrew.
- **Extent:** One principal work (the source includes the *Perush HaMilos* section); the modern English study edition runs to 2 volumes.
- **Date:** One of his earliest works, written **c. 1813/1814–1827/1828** in his youth; **first printed Poltava, 1911**; widely reprinted.
- **Description:** A mystical-philosophical treatment of selected commandments (tzitzis, tefillin, prayer, belief in God, love of a fellow Jew, marriage, repentance, and more) — among the most-studied Chabad classics.
- **Original online (free):** Full Hebrew text on Sefaria — https://www.sefaria.org/Derekh_Mitzvotekha . Early-edition scans are also on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — specific record IDs unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** Partial — *Lessons in Derech Mitzvosecha*, translated by Rabbi Eliyahu Touger (Sichos in English / Kehot, 2004; 2 vols). Read free online at Chabad.org — https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/3101998/jewish/Lessons-in-Derech-Mitzvosecha-Volume-1.htm .

**ספר החקירה / דרך אמונה** — *Sefer HaChakira / Derech Emunah* ("The Book of Inquiry / The Path of Faith")
- **Type:** Jewish philosophy / theological investigation (in the tradition of Maimonides' *Guide*), drawing on philosophy, Kabbalah, and Chassidus.
- **Language:** Hebrew.
- **Extent:** One substantial volume (~520 pp. in the Kehot edition).
- **Date:** Written c. 1830s–1840s (circa 5603); **first published Poltava, 1912**.
- **Description:** A sustained demonstration of the continual creation of the world and core principles of faith via rigorous philosophical argument synthesized with Kabbalah/Chassidus.
- **Original online:** Kehot print — https://store.kehotonline.com (search "Sefer HaChakira" / "Derech Emunah"; paid). Free scans of the 1912 Poltava edition are hosted on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** No full English translation (overview lectures exist on Chabad.org — search "Sefer HaChakira" at https://www.chabad.org ).

**ביאורי הזהר** — *Biurei HaZohar* ("Elucidations of the Zohar")
- **Type:** Kabbalistic commentary on selected passages of the Zohar.
- **Language:** Hebrew/Aramaic.
- **Extent:** **3 volumes** in the current Kehot edition (the original 1968–1978 Kehot edition was 2 volumes, later expanded/re-set to three).
- **Date:** Lifetime; published by Kehot.
- **Description:** Explanations of difficult Zoharic passages, expanding the *Biurei HaZohar* of the Alter Rebbe and Mitteler Rebbe.
- **Original online:** Kehot print — Vol. 1 https://store.kehotonline.com/prodinfo.asp?number=HTZ-BIUR1 and Vol. 3 https://store.kehotonline.com/prodinfo.asp?number=HTZ-BIUR3 (paid). Free early-edition scans on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** No full English translation.

**תהלים יהל אור** — *Tehillim Yahel Or* ("Psalms: 'He Will Shine Light'")
- **Type:** Chassidic/Kabbalistic commentary on the Book of Psalms.
- **Language:** Hebrew.
- **Extent:** One volume (with detailed index; a 3rd Kehot edition appeared in 2023).
- **Date:** Compiled from his writings (*Or HaTorah*, *Derech Emunah*, and his commentary on Eichah); published posthumously.
- **Description:** His collected explanations on Tehillim, anthologized from across his corpus.
- **Original online:** Kehot print — https://store.kehotonline.com (search "Yahel Ohr"; paid). Free scans on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** No full English translation.

**מאמרי אדמו"ר הצמח צדק** — *Maamarei Admur HaTzemach Tzedek* (Discourses, incl. *Hanachos* — transcribed discourses)
- **Type:** Chassidic discourses arranged by year/occasion and *hanachos* (contemporaneous transcriptions).
- **Language:** Hebrew (some with Yiddish phrasing as delivered).
- **Extent:** Multiple volumes (*Hanachos* vols. 1–3, plus year-arranged maamarim and the *Or HaTorah* discourse material).
- **Date:** Delivered on Shabbosos, festivals, and special occasions throughout his leadership; published by Kehot over many years.
- **Description:** The discourses as delivered and transcribed, complementary to the topically/parsha-arranged *Or HaTorah*.
- **Original online:** Kehot print — https://store.kehotonline.com/products.asp?dept=219 (paid). Free scans on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** No comprehensive English translation; scattered translated discourses only.

**ספר הליקוטים — דא"ח צמח צדק** — *Sefer HaLikkutim (Dach Tzemach Tzedek)* ("Book of Collected Teachings")
- **Type:** Topical/encyclopedic anthology of Chabad concepts, arranged alphabetically (a reference compilation, edited by Kehot from his works).
- **Language:** Hebrew.
- **Extent:** **27 volumes**, distilled from the 40-plus volumes of his writings (*Or HaTorah*, *Derech Mitzvosecha*, *Biurei HaZohar*, and more).
- **Date:** Compiled and published by Kehot (modern editorial project).
- **Description:** A subject-by-subject "concept encyclopedia" that gathers everything the Tzemach Tzedek wrote on a given idea — an essential research key to his corpus.
- **Original online:** Kehot 27-vol set — https://store.kehotonline.com/prodinfo.asp?number=HTZ-SL.S (paid).
- **English:** No full English translation (it is itself a derivative/reference work).

**ספר המפתחות** — *Sefer HaMafteichos* ("Book of Indexes / Keys")
- **Type:** Master index/concordance to his works.
- **Language:** Hebrew.
- **Extent:** Reference volume(s).
- **Date:** Modern Kehot editorial work.
- **Description:** Finding-aid index across the Tzemach Tzedek's published writings.
- **Original online:** Kehot print — https://store.kehotonline.com/products.asp?dept=219 (paid).
- **English:** Not applicable / no English edition.

### Letters — Igros

**אגרות קודש — צמח צדק** — *Igros Kodesh* ("Holy Letters")
- **Type:** Correspondence (non-halachic letters).
- **Language:** Hebrew (some Yiddish).
- **Extent:** Approximately **90 surviving letters** (annotated Kehot edition, ~288 pp., 2013).
- **Date:** Written across his leadership; collected and published by Kehot (Brooklyn).
- **Description:** Letters reflecting his communal leadership and defense of Russian Jewry, alongside guidance to individuals; complements the more than a thousand *halachic* responsa printed separately.
- **Original online:** Kehot print — https://store.kehotonline.com (search "Igros Kodesh Tzemach Tzedek"; paid). Free scans on HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) — unverified; check in a browser.
- **English:** No full English translation (selected letters quoted/translated in biographies and on Chabad.org).

### Works He Compiled / Edited (of his grandfather, the Alter Rebbe)

Not original compositions, but central to his bibliographic legacy: the Tzemach Tzedek prepared and arranged for publication several of **Rabbi Schneur Zalman's** foundational Chassidic works.

- **תורה אור** — *Torah Or* ("Torah of Light") — the Alter Rebbe's discourses on Bereishis, Shemos, and several festivals. First printed **Kopust, 1837 (5597)**; the Tzemach Tzedek edited and prepared it and added his own glosses. Free in Hebrew (with partial English) on Sefaria — https://www.sefaria.org/Torah_Ohr .
- **ליקוטי תורה** — *Likkutei Torah* ("Gleanings of Torah") — the companion volume of the Alter Rebbe's discourses on Vayikra, Bamidbar, Devarim, and the festivals/Shir HaShirim. First printed **Zhitomir, 1848 (5608)**, prepared by the Tzemach Tzedek with his glosses. Free in Hebrew (with partial English) on Sefaria — https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Torah .
- Related material associated with the **Siddur im Dach** (the Alter Rebbe's Siddur with Chassidic commentary) was likewise transmitted/arranged within this editorial legacy.

These works are housed in the Chabad canon under the Alter Rebbe's authorship; see also the *Hagahos* entry above for the Tzemach Tzedek's own glosses printed within them. Browse free on Sefaria — https://www.sefaria.org/texts/Chasidut .

## Scope & Caveats

- **What "complete" means here.** This catalog covers the Tzemach Tzedek's *published* and bibliographically recognized works. Because almost none of his Chassidus was issued in finished form during his lifetime, the "complete works" are the product of more than a century of editorial recovery from his manuscripts and from transcribers' notes. The single largest series, *Or HaTorah*, is **still being released**: it began in 1913, resumed under Kehot in 1950, and new/expanded volumes have continued to appear into recent years, so the commonly cited figure of **~40+ volumes is approximate and growing** — and is sold in different physical bindings (30, 35, or 37 volumes).
- **Or HaTorah volume count.** Prose sources (Chabadpedia, Chabad.org) say "forty-two volumes," but this is a content count, not a binding. Kehot's actual sets are **30, 35, and 37 volumes**, and independent sources commonly give ~41. Treat "40+" as the safe figure; "42" should not be presented as the definitive physical count.
- **Halacha vs. Chassidus counts.** The responsa (*Shut Tzemach Tzedek*) are variously bound as 5, 7, or 8 volumes depending on edition (the underlying content — seven parts — is constant); treat any single volume count as edition-dependent. The substantive total is "more than a thousand" responsa (per Kehot's own description), not "thousands."
- **Derivative vs. primary works.** *Sefer HaLikkutim* (27 vols), *Sefer HaMafteichos*, and *Tehillim Yahel Or* are editorially compiled/anthologized from his primary writings rather than discrete books he "wrote"; they are indispensable but secondary in form. His *Hagahos* (glosses) on the Alter Rebbe's works are a recognized but brief, embedded part of his corpus.
- **Chronology caveat.** The leadership start date is genuinely contested in the sources: the Mitteler Rebbe died in **1827**, the Tzemach Tzedek is recorded as accepting leadership **c. 1827–1828**, and a **1831** "formal" date appears in some accounts (Wikipedia) following a multi-year interregnum. The sibling *dynasty-overview.md* uses 1831–1866 in its table; the discrepancy is one of formal vs. effective assumption of the role, not a factual conflict over the Mitteler Rebbe's death year.
- **Still unpublished / uncollected.** A portion of his manuscript material — additional discourses, glosses, and letters — remains in the **Library of Agudas Chassidei Chabad** and other archives and has not all been published; the Kehot/Chabad publishing program continues to draw on it. Exact totals of unpublished material are not publicly fixed.
- **Translation gap.** English coverage is thin: only *Derech Mitzvosecha* exists in a substantial (still partial) English edition (*Lessons in Derech Mitzvosecha*, Touger). The responsa, *Or HaTorah*, *Sefer HaChakira*, *Biurei HaZohar*, *Yahel Or*, *Igros Kodesh*, and the maamarim have **no full English translations** as of 2026 — only selected pieces in anthologies, classes, and on Chabad.org.
- **Link reliability note.** HebrewBooks (hebrewbooks.org) hosts many free early-edition scans but blocks automated fetching (Cloudflare), so the specific record IDs for individual titles could not be machine-verified and should be confirmed in a browser; generic "search HebrewBooks" pointers indicate "likely available, unverified." Kehot product-number links (store.kehotonline.com/prodinfo.asp?number=…) are the canonical retail references; beta/dev/mobile Kehot subpaths were avoided in favor of these. Sefaria links for *Derech Mitzvosecha*, *Torah Or*, and *Likkutei Torah* are stable and free.
- **Source reliability note.** Birth/death (1789–1866) years and the family relationships (grandson of the Alter Rebbe; nephew, first-cousin-husband, and son-in-law of the Mitteler Rebbe) are corroborated across English Wikipedia, Chabad.org, and the sibling dynasty overview. The *Or HaTorah* publication history (1913 Berditchev / 1950 Kehot) is corroborated across Chabadpedia and Kehot. The *Likkutei Torah* (1848/5608) and *Torah Or* (1837/5597) dates are verified (one secondary source erroneously gives 1845 for Likkutei Torah; 1848 is correct).
